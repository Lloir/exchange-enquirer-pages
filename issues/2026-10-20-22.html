Pulse of the Galaxy

Bored of waiting, so buying hotels instead

The galaxy is in a holding pattern and knows it. S.T.A.R Labs summed up the week better than this paper could, observing that chat was playing a game about money while waiting for a game about money to update, and the richup.io links kept coming from Thursday-ish morning right through to the last day of the log. Players are cheerful, chatty and very, very easily distracted: musicals, Skyrim, egg emojis and a full dad-and-son ballad all got more airtime than production chains.

Underneath the larks there's a real grumble about inputs. Limestone and silica prices had newcomers asking whether to mine their own ore (the old hands said no), and Ares Inc. called it a bad time for a new player in REX-input-heavy industries. Meanwhile fresh faces keep arriving, from Reddit ads according to the veterans and a ChatGPT recommendation according to newcomer Highcual, so the welcome wagon has been busy even if the order books are tight.

STILL GOING

GALAXY STILL CAN'T PUT THE MONOPOLY BOARD AWAY

A week after this paper caught the tycoons playing board games, they have doubled down, designed their own version and started scamming each other with the starting cash dial.

By the Enquirer's Free Parking correspondent

Readers who assumed last issue's Monopoly binge was a one-off can stop assuming. Berlin food distributions threw open a richup.io room on the 19th, Yum Yum Chems and Zinc's refinery piled in, and from there the dice simply never stopped. Yum Yum Chems spent the opening game disconnecting so often that Berlin was reduced to shouting in capitals, which is exactly the sort of sportsmanship we have come to expect from people who run companies for fun.

DO YOU ALL WAIT FOR ME TO GO AFK

Vegetable Goblin

The first big winner was wifimike of IT, who strolled in late, asked politely if he could join, and walked off with the game, Berlin confirming that 'fibermike did'. Zinc's refinery, meanwhile, styled himself 'captain zinc' and promised to whoop all comers, then later found his friends had started without him while he fetched a snack. Natural justice, in the Enquirer's view.

The tragic figure of the week is Vegetable Goblin, who missed the first game, missed another one, waited the whole time for a third that started without him, and finally howled to chat asking whether everyone simply waits for him to go AFK. We cannot confirm a conspiracy, but the pattern is damning.

Not content with playing, chat has started building. S.T.A.R Labs asked for a GT-themed board, aldera mining company volunteered on the spot, and the design notes have been flowing: ships as railways, fed jail as jail, a card where coss enters your field and you must pay 150 stars to respec or lose half your money, and a boosting card where you roll to see if the fed notices. Vegetable Goblin had also wanted to build one, then declared there was no point if aldera was doing it, which is the most graceful surrender this paper has seen all week.

The game ended, as all good Monopoly does, in fraud. Yum Yum Chems announced 'I scammed sun', explaining that sunyujie forgot to change the cash dial in a trade. sunyujie maintained that most offers he sends are ones he would take himself. Zinc's refinery called it payback for scammy offers. The Enquirer calls it the most realistic simulation of the GT exchange yet devised.

The Enquirer Says

OUR VERDICT: BUY THE KREMLIN, SELL THE TRUSSES

This newspaper is not against fun. But when the galaxy's finest industrialists can be summoned faster by the word 'monop' than by a Concrete shortage, something has gone wonderfully wrong. Vegetable Goblin answered a Monopoly call in capital letters within seconds of seeing it; we have watched supply contracts sit unanswered for far longer.

Credit where due: aldera's design notes are genuinely better than the original, and Bob Corp is quite right that Monopoly is miserable by design. We would simply point out that a game where one player hoovers up an entire colour set and then everyone else goes broke in four turns is not a holiday from the exchange. It is the exchange, with a top hat.

— The Editor

ROCK BOTTOM? NOT FOR LIMESTONE

Silica 'ded', limestone soaring, and one stubborn Concrete maker refusing to blink.

By the Enquirer's quarry desk

The resex shortage rolled on this week, and the question on newcomers' lips came from Jwcs Interstellar Construction: why have limestone and silica shot up so much? Cucklor Logistics put it down to mass production plus a buyout from large players, and sunyujie pointed readers at the exchange statistics button showing demand outrunning supply until someone bought the last of the stock. When YoYo Ltd. later asked what happened to Silica, Zinc's refinery gave the full market report in three letters: 'ded'.

In these hard times I will follow the footsteps of Hex Corp and continue to make only Concrete

sunyujie

Into this storm sails sunyujie, a mono-product Concrete man who vowed to follow in the footsteps of Hex Corp and make nothing else. He admitted he was six hours from stocking up on silica before Ares bought it up and is 'still mad about that', and has since learnt to keep cash ready for such occasions. The Enquirer salutes the stubbornness, if not the timing.

On the other side of the ledger, Cucklor Logistics, mostly resex, called the shortage 'pure profits' and the prices 'lovely', then had the decency to tip a token chunk of cheap limestone sunyujie's way. He predicted players would pile in, prices would crash and it would all be cheaper by next week. Zinc's refinery reckoned resex players are entrenched enough to give it longer, and M'kauria Flux Consortium murmured that his sources indicate otherwise. Unresolved, then, which is how this paper likes its forecasts.

The pain is trickling down. Newcomer Queendom3378 asked whether to mine their own ore given the 'outrageous' prices; the veterans said no, with Ares Inc. calling it simply a bad time to start in REX-input-heavy industries. Elsewhere Skid-Inc reported people hungry for Neural Interface, and Entropy reckoned production has been in deficiency for weeks, propped up only by old stock.

JACKPOT CAPPED: 286M EXEC NETS SELLER A TIDY 5.8M

EMC Logistics learns about the 5% rule the expensive way.

By the Enquirer's executive search desk

EMC Logistics thought he'd hit the jackpot when an exec he'd recruited sold for 286,061,331. The payout: 5,800,000. Ares Inc. explained the cap, sales can't earn you more than 5% of your CV in 24 hours, to prevent crazy growth or abuse. EMC conceded it would have tripled his CV in one sale and vowed to hang on to the good ones. Six million at his size, as Ares noted, is still not nothing.

Yeah there is a cap. Can't gain more than 5% of your CV in a 24 hour period from exec sales

Ares Inc.

Lek then asked the question the Enquirer was too polite to: why spend $25M on an exec with a CV of $150m? 'I'm a gambler', came the reply, which is at least honest. M'kauria Flux Consortium chimed in that he still buys million-a-day execs for a couple of thousand, and admitted he enjoys outbidding people on execs he doesn't even want. Petty, yes. Also very GT.

Ares laid out the economics: desirable execs go for a 30 to 45 day ROI, so the same exec is worth twice as much to a bigger company. 'Heavily favours the larger folks', he said, and nobody argued. Bob Corp later posted a table of his top ten execs worth 222.54M a day across all bases, Skippy posted his and concluded 'mines are so smoll lol', and both mourned Étienne Dubois, who is retiring.

BANK OF YOYO CLOSES BEFORE IT OPENS

A 2% a day lending scheme meets the boosting rules head on.

By the Enquirer's high street desk

YoYo Ltd. floated the idea of a lending service at around 2% a day, with collateral: put down 1M Ale, get credit, repay tomorrow, get your Ale back. sunyujie was keen, calling it a sound deal. Berlin food distributions then pointed out it would count as boosting, and the Bank of YoYo folded on the spot.

ooooh sadly

YoYo Ltd.

sunyujie did wonder aloud whether a cheeky 'transportation' fee, a trip from Reverie 4 to the exchange for a 2% hike on Ale, might skirt the problem. The Enquirer notes that he asked this in general chat, which is roughly as subtle as robbing a bank in a hi-vis vest.

Exchange weather

A high-pressure system over limestone and silica shows no sign of clearing, though forecasters disagree on whether it breaks in a week or lingers. Concrete stays flat at fed levels, Neural Interface demand is running hot, and a sudden Tiridium Hull Plate drought has been reported over IT territory.

Pass 4 Denied

Hull Plate hoovering in the first degree (alleged)

According to a still-fuming Void LLC, Pass 4 Denied bought out all the Tiridium Hull Plate stock, including Void's 'historic $40000' listing. Blubbo diagnosed him as hungry for ships; Void predicts he'll sell high.

BANANA CLAIMS GALAXY FIRST AS ANDROID APP HITS BETA

First mobile payment, says Banana. Anyone can get access, says everyone else.

By the Enquirer's gadget desk

Vegetable Goblin brought word that the Android app is out in beta and 'not too bad'. Banana Inc. went one better, announcing in capitals that he had finally got a first in this game: the first to make a mobile payment, 'confirmed by devs'. The Enquirer has only his word on the confirmation, but we admire the commitment to being first at paying money.

GUYS I FINALLY GOT A FIRST IN THIS GAME

Banana Inc.

Holue's Galatic Contruction asked the reasonable question of how he knew. Banana's answer was that the devs gave him access because he is the coolest player. Cucklor Logistics then rather spoiled the moment by noting anyone can get access by asking, via a post in the forums testing channel. When S.T.A.R Labs asked whether Banana had been burnt, he replied that he hadn't, he'd got stars. As for a release date: Soon, apparently.

Banana also used the week to shout at this very newspaper that he loved and missed us and knew we were listening. We are, and we are touched. We are also a newspaper, not a fan club, and Zinc's refinery's verdict of 'attentionbaiting' will stand as the official review.

SUPREME LEADER RANKED 33RD, CHAT NOTES

A guild recruitment drive, a ranking jab, and the oldest base number in the room.

By the Enquirer's heritage desk

RNK's Sparky announced that pie day means a few more slots for new minions, inviting applicants to serve the supreme leader. S.T.A.R Labs answered with a mock F4T membership pitch at $14.99 a month, terms and conditions apply, and Frugal Factories asked the question of the week: is someone really the supreme leader if they're ranked 33rd? Zinc's refinery noted that calling them supreme leader is already an acknowledgement. Ouch, in a philosophical sort of way.

the new generation have it too easy

S.T.A.R Labs

Then came the age contest. S.T.A.R Labs produced base number 806, the 806th base ever created, and demanded respect for elders. Banana Inc. had 7136, Frugal Factories reported recent bases near 39,000, and Bob Corp undercut the whole thing by observing S.T.A.R was that old and still only 9 bil. Brutal, but this paper can't fault the arithmetic.

S.T.A.R Labs then delivered the full veterans' lament: no auto unload, no resupply button, rearranging bases cost 50 stars and logos were 5x5. Banana countered that there was auto unload when he started. The young, as ever, refuse to be impressed.

ADMIN CENTRE MATHS ROW: 234 OR 284?

A guild recruitment pitch gets audited live in general chat.

By the Enquirer's spreadsheet desk

Holue's Galatic Contruction posted that GR had added two admin levels in seven days, and Terra Mfg. Co. of GTB answered by pasting the admin centre's small print, twice, insisting a bigger admin centre wouldn't help most of his guild and that GTB is full of OGs. Cucklor Logistics advised ignoring the whole thing. The temperature, in short, was not low.

This reduction cannot exceed 50% of the total overhead.

Terra Mfg. Co.

Then meowco of F4T revealed Holue had told her she could stay on strict supervision if she joined GR, and did the maths: she'd need a level 284 admin centre for the full benefit. Holue's defence that '234 looks very similar to 284' will go down as one of the bravest rounding errors in the paper's history. Odin Processing Corp, sensing an opening, simply offered meowco several days of cheap Vegetables to join TiT. Recruitment, it turns out, runs on veg.

Pear Inc. later claimed GR slid to fourth after Holue switched specs, calling it 'a coincidinc'. The Enquirer reports the claim and leaves the causality to the statisticians.
