Pulse of the Galaxy

Sleepy start, unhinged finish

The day opened with chat taking its own pulse and finding none: 'talk abt a dead chat' before four in the morning, followed by a debate about whether anyone was outside touching grass. By late afternoon the TiT race had dragged everyone back indoors, and by evening the galaxy had dissolved into recycling-symbol walls, number spam and a word that may or may not be 'BLOSS'.

Underneath the noise, the practical questions kept coming: when do employees retire, how is a guild donation valued, where on earth do you find antimatter usage. Newcomers asked, veterans answered, and nobody got upset about it, which by this paper's standards counts as a heartwarming community moment. The real anxieties were small-guild ones: SWM looking abandoned, and one player pleading that the market is simply 'over produced to hell'.

PEAR GOES FOR BROKE: ONE BILLION ON EMITTERS

Last week's sofa casualty pours his entire fortune into a race ship, grabs the lead, then appears to suffer a mysterious and very loud setback.

By the Enquirer's pit lane correspondent

Readers of last week's Couch Surfing Edition will remember Pear Inc. coming a cropper on GP's golden sofa. This week he decided the answer to that humiliation was to strap a billion credits of emitters to a spaceship, which is the kind of recovery plan this newspaper lives for.

TOO LATE - 1 BILLION ON EMITTERS

Pear Inc.

Ahead of TiT's 'WE ARE SO BACK Race', Pear announced in capital letters that he had put shields on his ship, then threatened to add 5000 emitters, then confirmed he had spent all his money on the ship. When Sparky tried to interest him in a shiny production exec, Pear declared it was too late: the billion was gone. He also reported a building degradation figure of 505, which Farmy greeted with 'imagine 505% building degb'. We are imagining it. It hurts.

Astonishingly, it seemed to work. Farmy's 18:15 standings had Pear Inc. top at checkpoint 2/7, ahead of Flip Co and Berlin food distributions on 1/7. Murderous Trading Corps immediately predicted Pear would be 'blueshelled by berlin', which is either expert analysis or a man who has played too much kart racing.

At 20:33 Pear asked what he would actually get for winning. Farmy's reply was '00000'. Pear's reply to that cannot be printed in a family tabloid, though it was only a single finger.

Then, at 21:38, Berlin asked 'why did you reset? :(' and a minute later Pear erupted in a string of starred-out expletives including the word 'riestet', followed by what appears to be his face hitting the keyboard. Whether Pear's race is over, and who exactly reset what, the log does not say. The Enquirer notes only that this is the second week running Pear has ended up flat on his back.

47 PER CENT OF WHAT, EXACTLY?

Sparky lists a monster production exec and suggests buyers bulldoze their empires to afford him; the maths desk arrives to ruin the party.

By the Enquirer's executive recruitment desk

Sparky put Louis Fournier up for sale, billing him as one of the strongest production speed execs around with a 47% bonus. Farmy could not even hit the minimum bid, at which point Sparky offered the sort of financial advice that gets people sectioned from spreadsheets: knock down everything you own and sell it for this one man.

if you ask me, the smart choice is destroying all your buildings, selling at exchange and then use the money to buy him

Sparky

Sparky also waved away building degradation as 'nothing compared to what you get from the insane production speed', a bold stance on a day when Pear was nursing a 505.

Quiet Berm Solutions called 47% 'nuts'. Then the killjoys turned up. Echelon Interstellar Group pointed out the bonus is additive, not multiplicative, and Bob Corp reckoned it is worth about 9 to 10 research levels: a healthy boost at level 10 or 20, merely 'alright' at 70 and up. Whether Louis Fournier found a buyer is not recorded. Sparky's sales patter, the Enquirer judges, deserved better than arithmetic.

SHIELD WALL BLUES AS 'OVER PRODUCED TO HELL' WARNING SOUNDS

A rallying cry meets a reality check in the small hours.

By the Enquirer's commodities desk

In the early hours Banana Inc. demanded 'We need to get the shield wall bck to 15k!!', a call to arms the Enquirer admires for its exclamation marks if nothing else.

Odin Processing Corp was having none of it, warning that the stuff is 'being over produced to hell now guys, no way to keep buying it all up'. Nobody, as far as the log shows, managed to rebuild the wall. We suspect King Canute had better odds.

Elsewhere on the trading floor, Pass 4 Denied went looking for precisely one Quantum Research Data on Ae8, was sent to Lek, and came back empty-handed but cheerfully permitted to go to bed. The smallest order of the day, and the most wholesome failure.

And GreaVroes LLC revealed a cunning expansion strategy: settling more T2 worlds because the good execs simply refuse to work on T1 ones. Even the staff in this galaxy have standards, it seems.

Exchange weather

A billion-credit low pressure system over Pear Inc. is expected to bring scattered expletives. The shield wall remains stubbornly below 15k with persistent overproduction drizzle, while a warm 47% front drifts through the exec market, cooling sharply once anyone does the maths.

ALDERA'S NEW STRONGMAN SEIZES THE PRESS

Hale Emerson wins the presidency despite, by his own company's account, not being terribly good at things.

By the Enquirer's press freedom correspondent (while we still have it)

aldera mining company announced that Hale Emerson has been elected its new president, then immediately briefed against him in public chat, which is the sort of transition of power this newspaper finds deeply relatable.

you might have noticied, he is not the most competent of people and yet he won

aldera mining company

His first act in office, we are told, was to bring Aldera's press under the direct control of the president's office. The Enquirer would like to state, for the record and in case it ever needs to, that we are an unofficial fan tabloid and answer to no president, competent or otherwise.

Frugal Factories expressed surprise that 'that's the guy leading an $18 billion company'. Odin Processing Corp, meanwhile, went straight to the question on everyone's lips: do they retire at 70? Farmy reckoned around there. Some would call that hopeful.

SWM DECLARED 'DED' AS NEIGHBOURS PEER THROUGH THE CURTAINS

The feeder guild's future is questioned, with one former member offering an unflattering theory.

By the Enquirer's guild obituaries desk

Berlin food distributions admitted to being 'worried about the future of SWM' after Yum Yum Co. went asking TiT whether it could guest there. Farmy issued the verdict bluntly: 'SWM is ded'.

seems they've got worse since I left

Murderous Trading Corps

Murderous Trading Corps, who described SWM as a feeder guild, offered the time-honoured analysis of every ex: it's gone downhill since I left. Skye Blu Foods provided the more boring but probably accurate explanation that half the members did not seem to have been online recently.

The Enquirer is not yet writing the obituary. But we are sharpening the pencil.

GALAXY ANSWERS LIFE'S BIG QUESTIONS (AND SOME SMALL ONES)

Retirement ages, antimatter, guild donations and the meaning of everything, all sorted before teatime.

By the Enquirer's helpdesk

Wakawulawuha asked when regular employees retire. BlueCo said 60, Farmy said 70, and Medici Mining Conglomerate split the difference with 'probably random between 60-75 or something', which is how all the best science is done. Frugal Factories noted Maximilian Köhler is still going strong at 60, a ringing endorsement.

V.O.C asked how guild donations are valued and Bob Corp answered 'average price' in under a minute. Efficient. Suspiciously so.

港星製造發展有限公司 wanted to know where to find antimatter usage, and after Murderous Trading Corps offered the cryptic 'statistics', Make Stuff INC delivered the actual answer: click the material, then the pie chart next to the current price. The grateful 'ohhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh, thank you very much!' was the nicest thing said all day. The same player later asked for the answer to life, the universe and everything, and Banana Inc. duly supplied 42. Customer service in this galaxy is excellent, provided you ask the right person.

Less lucky was Gundercorp, who did not remember buying exec Aline Rocha and asked if agri players rated her. S.T.A.R Labs said keep her; Cracklor Logistics. said she was a spy and should go to the grinder. Her fate is unknown, but the Enquirer suggests she keeps her head down.
