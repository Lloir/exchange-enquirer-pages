Pulse of the Galaxy

Comfortably rich, quietly exhausted

Part II of this retrospective finds Andromeda settling into adulthood: the panic of the founding months has given way to spreadsheets, prestige perks and tycoons casually discussing billions the way they once discussed their first iron shipment.

The mood across these five months was less excitement than upkeep. More than one tycoon admitted the game had become a matter of maintaining what they already had rather than discovering anything new, and the galaxy's biggest flex of the period was less a victory than an endurance record.

THE GALAXY GROWS A MIDDLE AGE

Perks, planners and price caps: how Andromeda spent its second quarter

By the Enquirer's founding desk

If Part I was Andromeda learning to walk, this stretch was the galaxy learning to optimise. February brought Wishlists and a Base Overview tab (promptly switched off again within hours for technical issues, a humbling start to the quarter). March delivered the big one: Company Prestige Perks, rewiring how buildings, production, workforce and ships all behave, and tycoons spent the rest of the month arguing about which combination broke the game least.

By May the developers were adjusting Federal Reserve prices and recipe timings in the same update, the sort of housekeeping that only happens once a galaxy has enough history to need tidying. June closed the quarter with a new medal, more perk levels, and a developer admitting in public that a patch leak had definitely come from somewhere.

None of it was flashy. All of it mattered rather more than the galaxy seemed to notice at the time.

UNVERIFIED BUT WE BELIEVE IT

MEDICI CLAIMS GALAXY RECORD, DARES ANYONE TO BEAT IT

Early February saw the quarter's first proper flex, when Medici Mining Conglomerate announced a galaxy record for most buildings constructed in a single month and promptly challenged a rival to break it.

I claim galaxy record of most bldgs ever made in a month.

Medici Mining Conglomerate

This paper has no way to verify the claim against any official leaderboard, since none existed at the time, which did not stop it becoming the talk of the day regardless. That is, this paper submits, exactly how a proper tabloid record ought to work.

METALLURGY MARKET SHEDS A TRILLION OVERNIGHT

One company's crash becomes the whole exchange's problem

Early February brought what this paper is comfortable calling the quarter's biggest market event: a metallurgy crash so sharp that one trader reported losing a full trillion credits of company value compared to the previous month.

-1 trillion$ value this month compared to previous month

Lelouch Corporation

Chat's response was closer to admiration than sympathy. Price crashes on this scale were, by this point in Andromeda's history, treated less as disasters and more as weather: unpleasant, survivable, and occasionally worth betting on.

FED RESHUFFLES THE PRICE BOARD

May's update brought the quarter's largest single price adjustment, with the Federal Reserve nudging up the floor on everything from carbon to drinking water, and a sweep of recipe production times stretched out across the board, most notably a quarter-hour added to milk and nearly an hour tacked onto industrial machinery.

Price will rise, your profits will stay same don't panic if you're constructior

Builder Inc.

The developers were careful to insist nobody's actual profits would suffer, since selling prices were rising to match. Chat, as ever, reserved judgement until the next invoice came in.

TiT DECLARES ITSELF ABOVE THE REST OF THE UNIVERSE

One guild's pride spills rather generously over into everyone else's business

Guild pride reached a new and fairly unsubtle peak in early February, when a TiT member took to general chat to publicly rank his own guild above, in his words, the rest of the universe's plebeians.

I am going on record professing my admiration for them. Deal with them over the rest of the plebs on the universe

Coss

Whether this was meant as banter, genuine conviction, or simply a very confident Tuesday, the log does not say. What it does confirm is that nobody from a rival guild let the remark pass unchallenged for long, which this paper considers the real victory of the exchange: free entertainment, no production queue required.

INVESTIGATION ONGOING

PATCH NOTES LEAK EARLY; DEVELOPERS CONFIRM A SNITCH

Late June produced one of the quarter's better running jokes, after a tycoon correctly predicted an incoming update before it was announced. Pressed on how he knew, the developers' reply confirmed what half the galaxy had already suspected: somebody in the know is talking.

We have a snitch

Leki Fuel and Stuff

The identity of the snitch remains, as of this printing, entirely unconfirmed. This paper has its suspicions, but accuracy rule two forbids printing them without evidence.
