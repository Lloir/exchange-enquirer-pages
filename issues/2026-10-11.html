Pulse of the Galaxy

Sunday slow-cooker: chatty, multilingual and exec-obsessed

A long, lazy Sunday in general chat. The galaxy spent the morning on kebabs, falafel and fizzy drinks, the afternoon in four languages at once, and the evening watching an emoji parade (an egg, some popcorn, a panda) march past like nobody had anything better to do. Which, on the evidence, nobody did.

When chat did talk shop, it talked executives: who to bid on, when to bid, what to do when you already have 30 of them, and whether anyone will ever want an admin burden specialist. Limestone gave the traders a jolt mid-morning, but the real energy belonged to aldera mining company, who has discovered that this newspaper exists and has apparently decided to give it a constitutional crisis.

LORE

ALDERA STRONGMAN GRABS THE BALLOT BOX

Company president Hale Emerson funds his security forces, seizes control of elections and promises an A4 page of lore a day, and the Enquirer is apparently the paper of record.

By the Enquirer's foreign correspondent, reporting from somewhere behind aldera's checkpoints

Grave news from the aldera mining company, where a man called Hale Emerson appears to be running the place like a Bond villain with a mining permit. At 07:41 aldera announced that Company president Emerson had greatly increased aldera security forces funding, which in this paper's experience is never followed by anything good.

Protests agains this are being supressed by security forces

aldera mining company

Sure enough, by 16:43 the president had decreed that the executive council would no longer run elections, and that he would run them himself, 'for more secure elections and less voter fraud'. Within a minute, aldera reported that protests were being suppressed by security forces. The Enquirer notes the speed of events with professional admiration. Most real coups bother with a press conference.

aldera only learned of this newspaper's existence at 07:48 ('What paper'), after Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th pointed out that aldera had made it into print. Two minutes later the penny dropped, and by 07:53 aldera was promising 'a whole Word doc ammount of lore for the coming two or three days'. By 08:53 the lore was written and 'litteraly over an a4 long', and at 19:23 another A4 of it was promised for tomorrow.

We applaud the work ethic and we'll be reading every word. Readers should know, though, that the Enquirer reports on lore. It does not take dictation from presidents, however well funded their security forces are.

15 MILLION EXEC LEFT ON THE SHELF

Sparky is sure his age-42 executive is worth a fortune. The bidders, all staying very quiet, have other ideas.

By the Enquirer's auction-house desk

Sparky opened at 09:20 with a plaintive 'cmon guys', the timeless cry of a seller whose auction is going nowhere. Cracklor Logistics. said he was surprised anyone was paying 15m at all, and suggested that the lack of bids showed 'the real value'. As market analysis goes, that was brutal.

but i want him to shoot up now

Sparky

Medici Mining Conglomerate called the exec 'pretty good', then added 'Age 42 though', which is the auction-house version of 'lovely personality'. Sparky insisted the age 'doesnt matter' and said the exec could make him over 60m, so it should 'easily make 100-150m for someone with a good efficient planet'. Every estate agent in the galaxy just felt a wave of kinship.

Medici's advice was to wait, because prices usually shoot up in the last few minutes. Sparky's reply is our pull quote above, and patience does not appear on his list of skills. NebulaWorks explained that nobody likes to bid 24 hours early because the money stays frozen until the auction ends. Medici bids early anyway so he can find the exec again before he snipes it, and NebulaWorks just bookmarks them in gt-c.

The log never tells us the final price, so the Enquirer can't say whether Sparky got his 150m or a firm lesson in price discovery. We know which way we'd bet.

Page 3Business

LIMESTONE ROCKS THE EXCHANGE AS FLUX FIRMS DIG IN

One consortium refills its refineries and suddenly everyone is a geologist.

By the Enquirer's quarry correspondent

At 09:22 M'kauria Flux Consortium asked if someone could 'fix limestone please', as if the exchange took requests. Orthan Yates checked and found it 'trading high', then more than 20% up, and lamented owning only 100 of the stuff. The Enquirer sympathises. Missing a rally because you own a garden rockery's worth of the commodity is a special kind of pain.

>20% up, wish I had more than 100 limestone mined lol

Orthan Yates

Cracklor Logistics. said limestone was fine, told chat to drink the lime-ade, and remarked a few minutes later that flux production had spiked. He then wondered aloud whether the two might be related, while pointedly tagging M'kauria. Subtle, it was not.

M'kauria later admitted that the 'spike' was just a refinery refill, and said 8mil limestone was about the whole flux market, then revised that to around 11m. Earlier the consortium had complained that its flux contracts seemed to have vanished, and GreaVroes LLC kindly offered to go and make a bunch more. Somewhere in between, M'kauria thanked Cracklor 'most sincerely' for an unspecified favour, so it seems the limestone feud ended in a group hug.

MAKE STUFF MAKES STUFF: 600M IN A DAY

A personal best doubled, and chat's first instinct is to shout for a developer.

By the Enquirer's rich list desk

Make Stuff INC revealed at 19:32 that they had made 600M the day before, twice their previous best. Rather than demand a raise, they simply said they found it crazy. Refreshingly modest by general chat standards.

I find it crazy that yesterday I made 600M

Make Stuff INC

The reception was less dignified. Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th replied 'BOOSH' and tagged a DEV-tagged player with 'we got a boosher'. The Enquirer reads that as heckling from the cheap seats, not a formal complaint, and nothing more came of it. Congratulations to Make Stuff, who is now officially rich enough to be shouted at.

EXEC BAZAAR: ADMIN BURDEN STILL UNWANTED, SNICKERS BARS ACCEPTED

A busy day for the executive trade, where some people are a tough sell and others go for loose change.

By the Enquirer's headhunting desk

YoYo Ltd. opened the morning by promoting four executives on the exchange. Daniels Inc. added that admin burden specialists are 'a tough sell', and Medici and YoYo both agreed. If you specialise in admin burden, it may be time to update the CV.

Lektronic Industries had a revelation about Haruto Matsumoto, calling the executive 'insanely cracked' after realising the unused permit bonus could be a big deal. Berlin food distributions explained that it speeds up production on that planet based on how many free permits you have. The Spiffing Brit Co. learned that you do need to assign an exec to a base to get the bonus headhunting points.

At the bargain end, aldera mining company claimed to have bought an executive for '4 dollars and a snickers bar'. The Enquirer cannot confirm the exchange rate on confectionery, but if it's true, that's the deal of the weekend.

The Enquirer Says

THE ENQUIRER SAYS: NOBODY BIDS EARLY, SO STOP SULKING

Every auction this paper covers follows the same script. The seller panics at hour two, the buyers sit on their hands, and the price jumps in the final minutes. NebulaWorks put it plainly: nobody wants their money frozen for 24 hours. That isn't a conspiracy, it's just how people behave.

So, sellers, an empty bid list at breakfast doesn't mean the galaxy has rejected your 42-year-old executive. It means the galaxy is busy. Put the kettle on, bookmark the tab, and save the pleading for the last ten minutes, which is when it might actually work.

— The Editor

BERLIN ADMITS: 'I REALLY HAD THAT MANY'

Executive overcrowding scandal as the 30-exec limit turns out to be more of a suggestion.

By the Enquirer's prisons and staffing correspondent

Sparky innocently asked at 07:39 what happens if you win a bid when your executive roster is already 30/30. Chat's answers were a disgrace. Medici suggested murder, aldera proposed ritual combat, and YoYo Ltd. recommended just selling it to Fed.

it will become 31/30

Berlin food distributions

Then Berlin food distributions said calmly that the roster simply becomes 31/30. Pressed by a stunned Sparky, Berlin confirmed: 'yes, i really had that many'. The Enquirer has seen nothing so overcrowded since the last time the exchange crashed.

Nobody has made any formal complaint, and on Berlin's account it looks like a quirk of the system and not a crime. Still, this paper would like to know where executive number 31 sleeps.

PROPOSED

BANANA PLOTS GREAT CASH SINK HEIST

Banana Inc. puts forward a bold economic policy: all the money goes to Banana Inc.

By the Enquirer's fraud squad

When Berlin food distributions complained of needing more things to spend money on, Banana Inc. answered at full volume: 'WELL DO I HAVE A OPPOURTUNITY FOR U'. The opportunity was to send the money to Banana.

The plan is that every cash sink in the game reroutes its money to Banana, who would log on once a week to spend it, or perhaps subsidise the price of rations. Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th's review was a single 'no'. The Enquirer admires the honesty. Most schemes like this at least pretend to be a charity.

Wanted

Medici Mining Conglomerate

Incitement to executive-cide in the first degree

When Sparky asked what to do with a 31st executive, Medici replied 'Kill one of them' within the same minute, without a flicker of hesitation. He was later seen calmly advising on auction sniping strategy, as if nothing had happened.

Page 5Society & culture

TIT BAGS A STAR (WHO HAS NO IDEA HOW TO PLAY)

Farmy celebrates in capitals as S.T.A.R Labs joins the ranks.

By the Enquirer's astronomy and recruitment desk

At 09:56 Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th announced 'WE HAVE A' S.T.A.R Labs, then followed up with the capital-letters headline above. The Enquirer wishes to point out that this is our job, and we'd have done it with a pun.

TIT FINALLY CAPTURED A STAR

Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th

The new recruit's first contribution to the culture came at 13:02: 'no clue how to play it ngl'. That's honest, and likely to be popular with a guild that has just made it a mascot. S.T.A.R Labs later pitched a gacha system to the developers, came up with the idea only after executives arrived (make of that what you will), and by 19:20 had decided that both TiT chat and general chat were 'just nana and farmy'. Yum Yum Chems objected that they were there too, and that got them a mention in this paper. Well played.

BABEL BREAKS OUT IN GENERAL CHAT

Void translates the game into Spanish, and within the hour chat is speaking Italian, German and Bavarian.

By the Enquirer's translation desk (Google, mostly)

It started at 12:36, when Void LLC announced they had translated the game into Spanish, then admitted they were actually learning something. Berlin food distributions noted that a certain resource name came out as 'L'unico 1', and decided that translating into Italian had been a mistake.

the ui looks so weird in spanish

Void LLC

Chat took the rest from there. Italian pizza opinions were exchanged (pineapple, no; anchovies, si), Bob Corp offered 'mama mia!' and later 'weinerschnitzel, lederhosen!', and several players practised their German. Then Xip85 asked 'Wos fia a Sproch isn na des?', roughly 'what language is that, then?'. Woodstock Robotics guessed Bavarian, and Bavarian Industries was called in to confirm.

The verdict, in full and helpfully self-translated: 'Wos zum Deifl hobts na etz scho wida..', or 'What the hell is going on again?'. The Enquirer couldn't have summed up the afternoon better in any language.

MONSTER, V OR RED BULL? FLUX FIRM PICKS A SIDE

Cracklor asks a simple question about water, and chat ends up at war over energy drinks.

By the Enquirer's refreshments correspondent

At 07:58 Cracklor Logistics. asked how important drinking water is, 'not in game, just in general'. Within a minute M'kauria Flux Consortium had replied that it is less important than Monster energy drinks, with a hashtag claiming sponsorship that the Enquirer is fairly sure doesn't exist, and then added 'white monsters = white flux'.

monster is just syrup

Cracklor Logistics.

Cracklor declared himself 'a V guy' and gave the verdict above on Monster. Ginger beer then won near-unanimous approval, with Farmy loyal to one brand only and Yum Yum Chems a fan of 'ginger bear', which we assume is a different animal. 225 Manufactura closed the subject hours later with 'time to hydrate', so in the end the water lobby won.
