Pulse of the Galaxy

Sunday-sofa lazy, with one man screaming in capitals

This was a day off disguised as a game day. Most of the chat's energy went into things that aren't Galactic Tycoons at all: a Monopoly marathon that ran from teatime until nearly midnight, a detour into Zero Sievert for Sparky, and a long chat about browser scripts. When chat did talk about the actual game, it was mostly daydreaming. Murderous Trading Corps floated worker transport, space water parks and fuel-only empires one after the other, and Voidsteel Conglomerate shot each one down.

Underneath the silliness there's a familiar grumble about money: whether resex rewards wallets, Zinc's refinery being 'still broke tho', and EMC Logistics finding out the hard way that a rule he hadn't read cost him serious money. Players are relaxed, a bit cynical about the meta, and very happy to bankrupt each other on a pretend board.

GAME OVER

GALAXY'S TYCOONS SPEND SUNDAY PLAYING MONOPOLY

With all of space to run, chat chose a plastic board, a fight over a Paris square and one deeply unhappy lobby host.

By the Enquirer's board games and bankruptcy desk

At 18:28 S.T.A.R Labs asked if anyone wanted to play Monopoly and posted a richup.io link. Void LLC said yes and then, a minute later, seemed shocked that the offer was real ('oh shoot u were being fr'). Zenith industries signed up in two languages. The Enquirer was hoping for a quiet Sunday and did not get one.

GT players playing game about money

S.T.A.R Labs

By 19:14 sunyujie was claiming victory over S.T.A.R Labs. S.T.A.R Labs replied in capitals that he HAD NOT won, then offered '10k vitaqua at min'. sunyujie offered to save him from bankruptcy for 10m GT cash, which is the most Galactic Tycoons thing anyone has ever said during a different game. Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th laughed for a full line of HAHAHAs. At 19:16 sunyujie posted his own 'Game over! and the winner is... sunyujie' announcement. Nobody disputed it this time, and the paper is taking the silence as a concession.

S.T.A.R Labs summed it up best: tycoons playing Monopoly is 'like pilots playing flight sim'. Our verdict is that it's more like pilots crashing in flight sim and then asking for a rematch, because there were two. Berlin food distributions opened a second room at 20:19 after the first lobby broke up. S.T.A.R Labs asked why everyone was 'dropping a grand on paris', which is a fair question with no good answer.

The second game soon turned into a hunt for Yum Yum Chems. Berlin told him off for refreshing his page all the time, then let loose a row of capitals and keyboard-mashing at 22:08, and at 22:42 asked if he'd fallen asleep. Yum Yum Chems said he was moving back to his room. Meanwhile Farmy asked Frugal Factories why he was bidding on everything, and got no answer that the log records. Who won game two? The log doesn't say, and that suits a paper that likes its mysteries.

RESEX ROW: IS IT PAY TO WIN OR JUST PAY THROUGH THE NOSE?

Chat argued over whether the resource exchange rewards graft or wallets, and nobody changed their mind.

By the Enquirer's rock-tasting correspondent

GodfreLord started the day by saying the number one resex player is 'only' 125b, and that he has 25 ships to send out every day. Only a resex player could say 125 billion was a small number. He then did the sums: star slots on bases plus quest rerolls add up to about 12,500 stars to buy the lot, and he called star slots 'pure p2w'.

Yeah but p2w is not cool, especially in resex

GodfreLord

Not everyone agreed. Voidsteel Conglomerate said resex isn't very pay to win 'as long as you have ship slots available'. Cracklor Logistics gave the most sensible answer of the day: resex is a very dedicated spec that locks you into building for it, and agri is 'rex-lite for the wimps'. Murderous Trading Corps said rex 'is for lazy lumps', which is short and not very fair. Zinc's refinery reckoned that if you earn 100k a year you can just spend 1k a month on GT. The Enquirer keeps out of anyone's bank balance and will only say it's a bold budget.

The real business news came from Bob Corp, who told GodfreLord he sometimes buys his rocks and finds them poor quality. Skippy argued that Deutsche Metalle AG's rocks 'do taste good'. Customer reviews on the exchange have never been this personal.

READ THE SMALL PRINT

EXEC SALE GOES WRONG: EMC SAYS $200M SLIPPED AWAY

A recruit worth hundreds of millions, a CV cap nobody read about, and a profit of five million.

By the Enquirer's small print department

Just before midnight, EMC Logistics said the game had 'scammed' him out of $200m. He'd recruited an exec who turned out to be worth hundreds of millions, and someone paid that much for them. But his CV wasn't high enough to collect the full sale price, so he kept only $5m. He admitted he should have read the fine print first, and the Enquirer gives him credit for owning up while it was still raw.

No this was the other day, I got the exec from the recruitment but turned out they were worth hundreds of millions, which someone paid but my only profit was $5m

EMC Logistics

Lek asked the obvious question: had EMC spent about a seventh of his CV on execs that day? EMC said no, it happened the other day. That makes it a slow-burning disaster, not a fresh one. The paper notes that the money did leave someone's account. Where it went beyond EMC's $5m, the log doesn't say.

The Enquirer Says

THE ENQUIRER SAYS: THE SMALL PRINT IS STILL PRINT

This newspaper is all for print, small or otherwise, so it has some sympathy for EMC Logistics, who found out that a CV cap can turn a nine-figure exec sale into a five-million tip. Calling it a scam is going a bit far, though. A rule you didn't read is still a rule, and the game did warn him, quietly, in that grey text that every tycoon scrolls past.

Our advice to readers: before you sell anything worth more than your whole company, read the bit at the bottom. Then read it again. Then maybe ask Lek, who managed to start working out your CV before you'd finished complaining.

— The Editor

PLAN TO SHIP WORKERS BETWEEN PLANETS SINKS UNDER ITS OWN WEIGHT

Murderous Trading Corps had a stream of ideas. Voidsteel Conglomerate had a stream of objections.

By the Enquirer's wild ideas desk

At 12:49 Murderous Trading Corps asked: what if you could move workers between planets, with one housing planet supplying all the others? Voidsteel Conglomerate called it silly, then designed it anyway, with a 25% production penalty, citizens weighing one tonne each and manual shipping every cycle. Murderous admitted he 'never said it was a good idea'. That's an honest pitch, and the Enquirer respects it.

25% reduced production and each citizen weighs 1 ton

Voidsteel Conglomerate

Next idea: community projects, such as a space water park to cheer up workers. Voidsteel pointed out it would cost vitaqua to run. Fair enough. Even fun runs on the supply chain round here.

At 16:10 came the third idea: fuel bases everywhere, making nothing but fuel. The Cat Empire said it 'would be cool'. Vagabond suggested bases with just storage that you ship to. GreaVroes LLC went further and imagined mobile refuelling ships selling 'fresh space juice at rim-prices'. YoYo Ltd. said earlier in the day that he'd had a similar idea before, but the calculation, resupply and transport were hard. Three ideas, no buildings, one great phrase. Space Juice at the Rim would make a cracking album title.

SCRIPT CLINIC: SKIPPY MEETS TAMPERMONKEY

A user-script install becomes a group project, and the Sleek UI picks up a fan club.

By the Enquirer's IT helpdesk (have you tried turning it off and on again)

Skippy asked how to install user scripts, because the link just took him to a page of code. He then installed Tampermonkey but couldn't see the scripts in the game. Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th's guess was that the scripts weren't switched on, and he asked, with growing feeling, which scripts. wifimike gave the age-old advice to reload the page. Skippy announced 'i got it', and the helpdesk queue moved on.

Farmy then pushed the Sleek UI script as 'pretty amazing' and suggested the chicken chaos theme. wifimike called it the best UI around but said he'd turned it off. After nearly a year on the normal layout he clicks things without thinking, and he can't run plugins on every device he plays on. Skippy agreed it looks nice but takes getting used to. The Enquirer is pleased to report a tech support thread that actually ended with someone fixing their problem.

ALDERA CLIMBS TO THIRD IN BASE COUNT, EXPECTS TO STALL

A podium place, a rival's cheeky offer, and an honest forecast.

By the Enquirer's league tables desk

aldera mining company announced at 22:10 that it had reached rank three in base count, behind only AeriCore Industries and Aquaman. Berlin food distributions said 'nice' in between Monopoly outbursts. That's the most polite thing he said all evening.

EggInASac offered to 'throw up 5 more' bases to give aldera extra motivation, which counts as a threat and a gift at the same time. aldera expects to be stuck at 55 to 56 bases 'for a LONG time'. The Enquirer likes a tycoon who manages expectations, and that's rarer in this chat than a sensible Monopoly bid.

Also spotted: newcomer Highcual asked for starting tips, and MLE sent him to the help channel, where advice won't get buried in nonsense. Considering what filled the rest of the day, that was good advice.
