Pulse of the Galaxy

Chatty, cheerful and quietly obsessed with executives

Friday was not a day of feuds. The heaviest traffic was price-guessing at the exec auction house, and by mid-afternoon half of general had an opinion on what Thea Wagner was worth and the other half wanted an exec named after a game-show host or a Simpsons cameo. Nobody was jailed, nothing caught fire and nobody threatened anybody, which is bad news for a tabloid's sales figures.

Underneath the jokes there was some grumbling. Resource extractors were feeling hard done by: GodfreLord called resex the worst thing to do in the game, wifimike said his planets had hit the point where expanding means spending money to lose it, and Vagabond was already mourning the end of the starter multiplier. Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th also noticed how quiet general gets when Europe goes to bed. Chat's mood was cosy, slightly ground down and very easily distracted by a funny name.

UNVALUED

THEA WAGNER WORTH A BILLION, OR TWENTY, SAYS EVERYONE AT ONCE

Rearden Steel rolls the exec of a lifetime and general chat comes up with four different prices for her inside five minutes.

By the Enquirer's auction house desk

At 14:00 Rearden Steel announced the "hands down best roll I've had yet": Thea Wagner. Lek called her a beast within a minute, and from there the valuations got sillier.

or keep her - easy 20b over 2 months

Pear Inc.

Sumran Consortium said sell her, "easy 1b+". Pear Inc. said keep her, because she was good for 20b over two months for anyone deep enough into consolidated ops. Lek said 2b, maybe. Zinc's refinery called 1b nonsense and put her at 3b. That makes four experts, four prices, and a range wide enough to park a freighter in. This paper has seen better-calibrated horoscopes, and it writes its own.

Lek was the only one to show any working. He said one extra permit for consolidated operations "isn't that much" and a decent burden reduction is probably better, unless your company is very wide. S.T.A.R Labs asked whether the permits disappear when the exec retires. Zinc's refinery said "ofc", and the 20b dream deflated a little.

Then came Magnus Karlsson. Bob Corp admitted he had wanted to bid on him purely for the name, Frugal Factories put his production speed down to practising speed chess, and at 14:12 Zinc's refinery asked whether Magnus had just jumped from 30m to 220m. BlueCo answered "Indeed". A sevenfold rise in a few minutes is the closest this newspaper has come to a celebrity scandal all week.

There was also a public works project. Lek explained that his auction house script lets players right-click an exec's name in chat to link it. When Bob Corp complained he could no longer link Eva, Lek promised to add it "real quick". At 14:46 he told chat it was "not yet live though, give me a few minutes". The log doesn't say whether it went live, and Rearden Steel never said whether Thea will be sold or kept. The Enquirer is waiting impatiently for both.

SABOTAGE ALLEGED

CHICKENS DOWN TOOLS AS AUTO-UNLOAD FAILS AGAIN

Kentaurus Ballistics reports cargo left on the docks, a superstitious crew and poultry alleging a conspiracy.

By our maritime and livestock correspondent

At 03:34 Kentaurus Ballistics filed the most dramatic logistics complaint this paper has ever printed. Another ship had auto-unload turned off, the crew were getting superstitious, and the ship's chickens reckoned it was a crew conspiracy to discredit their Ship's Chicken status. The plant manager is retiring next year, which apparently is the only thing that saved them from being fired into space.

The chickens are refusing to leave their roosts

Kentaurus Ballistics

Medici Mining Conglomerate, who has never needed auto-unload, recommended a 1.5+ day buffer. Kentaurus said logic would suggest a bigger safety margin, "but that would be... logical". Zinc's refinery's verdict was "skill issue". Rearden Steel said he'd also like to space a few of his workers, which the Enquirer is choosing to read as solidarity.

Kentaurus admitted he burns cash like it's tainted, refuses to reset and quite likes a spread of production as protection against price swings. Medici also never reset, so at least he isn't alone. The chickens' dispute had not been settled when the log ended.

Page 3Business

WATER BARON: ANY MARKET I TOUCH WILL CRASH LIKE THE TITANIC

wifimike pumps 60 million a day, keeps his prices low on purpose and says that's why he's sticking to water.

By the Enquirer's utilities desk

GodfreLord asked a reasonable question: how was wifimike mining more than him with a smaller CV, and what level were his pumps? The answer was "uhh" followed by "...idk?". Medici Mining Conglomerate filled in that he has at least three large, always thirsty buyers. wifimike then said he was up to 60 mil a day. GodfreLord said he had 1 mil in total.

i know for a fact that any market i touch will crash harder than the titanic, which is why i havent done anyhting other than water for now

wifimike

Deutsche Metalle AG said he'd expected more than 60 million litres and told him to raise his prices. wifimike said no. He likes keeping water cheap and available even if he doesn't make much from it, he works mostly on request, and he sells on-planet so his ships don't limit him. A Galactic Tycoons player turning down profit out of kindness is front-page material in this paper.

Then the warning. Any market he touches, he said, will crash harder than the Titanic, which is why he's only done water so far. GodfreLord's reply, "Omg pls no", spoke for every producer listening. wifimike promised that anything new would be done the same way, which reassured precisely nobody.

HALF A CV IN VITAQUA? 'THAT'S JUST WHAT'S ON THE MARKET'

S.T.A.R Labs' fizzy water portfolio comes under public scrutiny.

By our liquid assets team

At 12:13 Pass 4 Denied pointed out that half of somebody's CV was Vitaqua, and Odin Processing Corp followed up that "Homie has 4.8B" in the stuff. S.T.A.R Labs owned up with the excuse that it was just how much he had on the market. Odin replied "lmfao", and so did the Enquirer.

Elsewhere SemEx reported selling a worker for 50m and receiving 22m. He followed it with "lol", which is braver than this paper would have managed. Over at the newcomers' end, sunyujie asked whether the exchange average price includes contract prices. Zenith industries said he "wouldn't assume so", and that was the most careful answer anyone gave all day.

Exchange weather

Water levels stay high and prices stay low under the wifimike front, with a severe Titanic warning for any market he goes near next. Vitaqua is pooling heavily around one TiT-adjacent lab. Exec auction prices are very unstable, with a Magnus front climbing from 30m to 220m in minutes.

Page 4Society & culture

RESEX 'WORST THING IN THE GAME', RIM WORLDS 'ALWAYS' LEFT OUT

Extractors moan about exec debuffs, and REX nostalgia sneaks in.

By the Enquirer's grievances desk

The resource extractors spent the morning feeling hard done by. Daniels Inc. said he learned the hard way that factory speed does nothing for resex. wifimike complained that the tier 1 abundance perk is always paired with "cannot work on rim worlds", and all of his worlds are rim worlds. Medici Mining Conglomerate called it the debuff that confuses him. This paper has no idea whether the balance holds up, but it does like a well-organised complaint.

GodfreLord then said resex was the worst thing to do in the game, "maybe only construction is worse". Space Inc. missed the days when REX was the best thing to do, Holue's Galatic Contruction said REX doesn't seem too bad, and GodfreLord called it "definetly the most pay2win thing in the game". So in the space of three minutes he'd told one group they were in the worst job and another that they'd paid to win. Very efficient.

For the newcomers' page: Jwcs Interstellar Construction asked how important it is to give employees optional consumables. wifimike said yes, Zinc's refinery said yes and then no, and Sparky said it's very important while you have the starting bonus. Chat managed to give one clear answer and one contradictory one, which is about par for advice in general.

FOUND

THE MAN WITH EVERY PLANET CHAT

S.T.A.R Labs hides some numbers in a random planet chat. Berlin finds them in under an hour.

By our surveillance correspondent

At 19:32 S.T.A.R Labs told chat he had posted a series of numbers in a random planet chat. Pass 4 Denied guessed it was Banana 1, "It always is". S.T.A.R said "nuh uh" and Cracklor Logistics. said "ya huh", which is roughly how most debates in general go.

At 20:21 Berlin food distributions posted that he'd found it. Asked what anyone expected, Berlin explained he joined all the planet chats months ago. Make Stuff INC and Cracklor Logistics. both pointed out that Berlin has all the planet chats. The Enquirer suggests anyone hiding anything in future picks a different hobby.

For the record, Banana Inc. had already used the small hours to ping SRSM'kauria Flux Consortium three times, explain a base on Seyfert with the words "Iron Ore" and "enhanced mineral water", and later invent the word "oddctopus". Nobody asked him to explain any of it.

Wanted

Zinc's refinery

Reckless borrowing in a rival galaxy

After being told about Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th's own game, Stellar Foundry, he was 124k in debt in it within minutes, found the bank, "just borrowed the max", and posted a net worth of 15.6k against 225k of debt. Back in Galactic Tycoons he was also seen valuing Thea Wagner at a confident 3b.

Reward: One debt-free starter base and a stern lecture on compound interest
