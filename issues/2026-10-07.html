Pulse of the Galaxy

Auction-addled, guild-hopping and quietly salty

If one thing ran through Wednesday it was the executive auction house. Players spent the small hours posting their rolls, the morning comparing admin burden traits, the afternoon finding out about the payout cap the hard way and the evening confessing to bidding up rivals for sport. Voidsteel summed up the general fatigue when he said the feature wastes way too much of his time, and then kept bidding anyway.

Underneath the exec mania sat a run of guild musical chairs: Coss back in favour with TiT, a TiT member poached by GP, DrD out the door and bouncing off two applications. Add a farewell from Waffles, a returning player asking what he'd missed and an hour of Rubik's cube boasting, and you get a playerbase that is busy, a bit restless and very much still talking. The 09:33 lull, when Makuria declared the chat empty, didn't last long.

CAPPED

CAPPED IN THE ACT! EXEC SELLERS DISCOVER THE FIVE PER CENT RULE

An executive goes for 458m, its seller gets 110m, and the auction house quietly keeps the change.

By the Enquirer's small print desk

Galactic Tycoons' shiny new executive auction house claimed its first high-profile victim before breakfast, when pack of salmon reported that his executive had sold for 458m but only 110m had actually landed in his account. Was it a bug? Sadly for him, no. Holue's Galatic Contruction explained that sellers can only receive a maximum of 5% of their current CV, otherwise smaller players could just flog an exec for a billion and skip the grind. Sensible, and also exactly the sort of thing you'd like to hear before clicking the list button.

That would have been nice to know before listing my executive, wouldn't it?

pack of salmon

S.T.A.R Labs asked where the missing 300 mil goes. "Gone," said Holue, with the bedside manner of a bailiff. Dumb pointed out that the cap is shown before listing, and that his own maximum is 14m however high the bidding goes, which this paper reads as a gentle reminder that the window was right there all along. Cracklor offered the day's most quotable economics: the cap is "making the rich poorer and the poor poorer too".

The saga flared again at teatime. Pickle Rick arrived in full keyboard-mash mode, conceded the cap is the anti-boosting measure, but argued that it was targeting him despite him clearly not boosting. Later he sharpened the point: tying the cap to CV means those who already boosted get a higher ceiling, which he reckons penalises the honest. The Enquirer notes this is the most coherent complaint anyone filed all day.

Meanwhile Voidsteel Conglomerate announced that Friendly Farmers, CV 165m, was set to get 148m from an auction, a casual 100% CV bonus. Bob Corp promptly accused him of casually spreading misinformation and pasted the rules in full, which confirm payouts are capped at a percentage of company value at listing, with the overspend going to the auction house. sunyujie then produced a screenshot showing a possible payout of 7,200 to 27M on a 555M CV, which rather settled the 5% question. Voidsteel, for the record, says the listing window doesn't show it for him. Mittcb's theory: special rules for Void.

WAFFLES SERVES ONE LAST CHEAP IRON BREAKFAST AND LEAVES

A farewell dump, a cursed company value and a chat left asking why.

By our departures lounge correspondent

At 04:04 Waffles Concrete Surplus announced a "last hoorah", dumping a couple of million units of super cheap iron on the market for one final time before saying goodnight. Zinc's refinery thanked the bossman; Skippy lamented that he only had $1m cash to take advantage, and SL Refining kindly offered to top him up.

last hoorah. dumped a couple mil super cheap iron on market again. goodnight all hope you guys have fun!

Waffles Concrete Surplus

Echelon Interstellar Group then noticed Waffles' final CV stood at a devilish $6.666B and declared him the winner. Waffles insisted it was an accident but said he'd take it, adding that reading chat with his morning coffee had made the game the most fun. A rare gracious exit, and the Enquirer will miss the cheap iron more than it will admit.

Medici Mining Conglomerate said he stopped buying when he saw what was happening, and Holue later asked why Waffles had deleted his account. The log doesn't say, and neither shall we.

The Enquirer Says

OUR VERDICT: READ THE LISTING WINDOW

The Enquirer has every sympathy for pack of salmon, who watched 348m vanish into the auction house's pockets. But as Dumb noted, the range is printed before you list. The cap exists to stop small companies flogging an exec for a billion and skipping the grind, and on that score it is doing its job.

That said, Pickle Rick has a point worth taking seriously: if the ceiling scales with CV, then a company that already padded its CV gets a higher ceiling. This paper doesn't make the rules, but it does notice when a rule pays out most to the people it was built to stop.

— The Editor

ADMITTED

SPITE BIDDERS CONFESS: 'I MAKE A SPORT OUT OF IT'

The exec auction house is apparently less a marketplace than a grudge match.

By the Enquirer's auction room sketch artist

Who needs a feud when you've got a bid button? Voidsteel Conglomerate admitted he has bid on a few execs he doesn't need, simply because he feels person x shouldn't be able to pay less than y, and that when certain players are bidding below 100m he "might click the button a few times". Drunkenduo's Ruthless Dividend, who has been in plenty of bidding wars already, cheerfully filed himself under "one of the idiots". Honesty, at least, is free.

I make a sport out of it to increase the price of executives begin bought by ats and gp

Pass 4 Denied

Then, at 21:18, Pass 4 Denied went further, announcing he makes a sport of pushing up the price of executives being bought by ATS and GP. Skippy's response was to suggest he keep his eyes peeled on TiT guild chat, which this paper finds more ominous than any amount of bidding.

Elsewhere Skippy observed the Dev bidding on executives as if he couldn't just magically make one appear, and Bob Corp said he was simply waiting for an epic crash out from someone outbid at the last moment. Berlin food distributions came closest, reporting he was sniped on one exec at the last second. Pass 4 Denied owned up to that one as well. Busy man.

Wanted

Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th

Selling chickens at maximum price, with alleged intent to boosh

Advertised 4000 special K2 chickens a day at $600, sold the lot to Pass 4 Denied, and was promptly accused by Zinc's refinery of a boost over 2.4m in 4 days, with Zinc claiming it was screenshotted and reported. Farmy maintains that max price doesn't mean booshing, Pass 4 Denied says chickens are just in high demand, and the case remains unresolved.

Reward: One special K2 chicken, $600, no questions asked

ECHELON'S TURNIP RUN PAYS OFF AT 16M A DAY

A ship was waiting, an executive was won, and the rest of chat did sums.

By the Enquirer's headhunting desk

Echelon Interstellar Group pipped Drunkenduo to Abena Adebayo, who is now headed to Tidal 1 for $141,476,862. Cracklor noted Echelon had a ship waiting, which is the kind of preparation the Enquirer usually only sees from people fleeing the scene. Zinc's refinery called it mid; Echelon called it $16M a day of profit and definitely worth it, and on those numbers we're inclined to side with the man with the spreadsheet.

$16M/day profit, definitely worth it

Echelon Interstellar Group

The secret, Echelon explained, is worker admin burden reduction once you're over the overhead cap, which adds up when you have 3.5M workers. He also says he is making $182M a day from con ops, against Cracklor's 111m, while Holue's view that con ops isn't really worth it was left looking rather lonely.

Drunkenduo then landed Adèle Valmont for a quarter of a billion and an estimated +$22m a day, though moments later he was wondering if she was not as good as hoped. Holue had earlier voiced suspicions of foul play over her price; nothing in the log backs that up, and Cracklor's main contribution was to note she can sing.

SMALL COMPANIES NOW 'SUB 2B', OR POSSIBLY MEASURED IN WATER

Plus discount herbs, sinking engines and a 3m bargain.

By our market stall correspondent

Skippy noticed that Sumran Consortium's definition of a "small company" keeps creeping up, now standing at sub 2B. Sumran mused it might be time to go to 3B, then offered all companies bigger than him contracts at market price plus 10%, before finally revealing that company size is actually measured by water production. Clear as mud, as policy statements go.

In the bargain bin, 热狗堆肥游乐场 offered Herbs at $89 against a quoted market price of $97 to any handsome men or beautiful women. Skippy asked whether he could be handsome without buying any, and was told yes, the most generous ruling of the day. Orb, meanwhile, grumbled that engines have tanked in price since he started making them, a curse every new producer eventually learns.

On the exec front, Skippy reported spending 3m on an executive earning 2.1m a day on his Copper Wire base, Rearden Steel reckoned a well-chosen exec could save 30 to 40M a day on a big T4 base, and Pear Inc. said his execs save him 200M+ a day. Voidsteel replied "Rookie numbers", naturally.

Exchange weather

Heavy bidding fronts over the auction house, with gusts of spite from the ATS and GP directions. Iron briefly flooded overnight following Waffles' farewell dump, engines remain depressed, and Herbs are forecast a touch cheaper than market for anyone handsome.

REJECTED

DRD OUT OF TIT, KNOCKED BACK BY ATS, TOO SLOW FOR GP

One agri 69 farmer, three guild doors, and a lot of shouting in between.

By the Enquirer's guild transfer window

It started with a poaching. Skippy of GP picked up a TiT member off the leaderboard, Drunkenduo's Ruthless Dividend demanded to know why GP hadn't picked him instead, and within minutes a row with Pear Inc. had escalated into the sort of language this family newspaper declines to print. By 21:44 DrD was announcing he was looking for a nice guild, preferably top 1, armed with a CV line of level 69 agri.

i'm the biggest TiT hater in the galaxy

Tato Industries

Yum Yum Co. invited him to the Swarm; DrD says Swarm then didn't talk to him. He applied to GP, but Skippy said GP was full and not in need of an agri player. He then aimed at ATS, where Hydrogenica Energies mentioned "this weirdo applying" before explaining he was busy planning a base. DrD declared the reply disrespectful and filed a complaint with Bob Corp. Hydro, ever the professional, directed him to the HR guy and his lawyers.

The verdict finally came from Tato Industries. Asked directly whether he'd rejected him, Tato replied "of course", later adding that he couldn't tell the difference between Pear and Farmy. Holue, ever helpful, pointed out DrD has four bases, and Pear accused Holue of joining in with the bullies. As of the last message DrD remains guildless and, in his own words, very shy. The Enquirer has its doubts on that last bit.

THE GREAT PLAGUE OF COSS: WHICH NARRATIVE ARE WE GOING WITH?

A metallurgy villain is welcomed home, and nobody can agree on the script.

By our metal industry obituarist

Coss greeted Bob Corp with "Welcome back Bob", and Skippy immediately asked whether Bob would let Coss back into ATS. Bob said he never had a problem with Coss, then asked which narrative everyone was going with, offering "Coss is destroying Metallurgy" as an option. Skippy vetoed it. The Enquirer admires a man who checks the script before the scene starts.

Coss killed the metal industry, yes. The great plague of Coss

Ares Inc.

Ares Inc. was having none of the reconciliation, declaring Coss killed the metal industry. Frugal Factories asked how, if it's so dead, Ares had won the metallurgy medal five months running. Ares ruled this not important. Coss, for his part, insisted he is just learning and trying to have fun, which Ares answered in capitals: DON'T LISTEN TO HIM.

As for where Coss actually ended up, Drunkenduo said TiT took him in because TiT is literally in his profile pic. Destiny, apparently, comes in avatar form.

CUBE CLAIMS SPIN OUT OF CONTROL

General chat briefly becomes a speedcubing forum and nobody stops it.

By the Enquirer's puzzles editor, still on two sides

Infinite Margin wandered in with a polite good evening and was soon comparing solve times with Berlin food distributions, admitting he never got below 30 seconds and that the Dodecahedron solves exactly like a 3x3. Berlin then claimed eleven and a half seconds on a 7x7. The Enquirer reports the claim and wishes him well with it.

The rest of chat set a gentler pace. Yum Yum Co. reported 55 seconds on a 3x3, Murderous Trading Corps never bothered, Cracklor has had one on the go since about age 10, Bob Corp asked whether anyone could solve a 0x0, and Farmy claimed 0.1 seconds, which we'll file alongside his chicken pricing. Pevilot SA spoke for the silent majority: why was he reading maths now?

Berlin, between cubes, also revealed he had 69 execs listed on auction and at one point held 27 out of 26 execs. The cube might be the least scrambled thing he owns.
