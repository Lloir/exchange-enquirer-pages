Pulse of the Galaxy

Dead at dawn, deafening by lunch

The day opened with a wake. Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th declared general chat and even TiT chat dead overnight, S.T.A.R Labs reported a grand total of 10 messages in F4T, and Sumran Consortium counted 4 in ATS, calling it nearly a new record. By 13:00 the problem had reversed entirely, with TiT holding a full Amy Winehouse singalong in public and Voidsteel Conglomerate proposing a posting quota.

Underneath the noise there is a whiff of growth and restlessness. Pear Inc. watched the active player count tick from 790 to 813, S.T.A.R Labs announced F4T was hiring, TiT went shopping for a manufacturing specialist, and Farmy summed it up with "everyones hiring". The day closed with a polite little queue of newcomers saying hello, which is more manners than the rest of the log managed all afternoon.

DESCHAMPS BY A MILE: NAO ITO HELD TO TWO VOTES

Aldera's election ends in a 1.4 million vote landslide, a vote-stuffing cry, a disputed ballot and an investigation that found absolutely nothing.

By the Enquirer's returning officer

aldera mining company opened the morning by scolding this very newspaper for failing to cover the sudden departure of one Emerson from Aldera's ranks, then promptly handed us something far juicier: election results. Sébastien Deschamps took 1.4 million votes, Blake Sterling 72,040, Vega Karr 32,219 and Nao Ito, bless him, 2. The Enquirer has seen closer contests between a chicken and a pie machine.

and its a sweeping victory for deschamps

aldera mining company

The sympathy vote arrived immediately. Zinc's refinery mourned "nao ito my boy" and reckoned the only two ballots were his own and Nao's, before Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th revealed he had voted for Nao too, on the grounds that Nao is a former employee. aldera then ruled that Farmy has no right to vote in Aldera elections, briefly dropping Nao to a single vote. Being disenfranchised and a party to election drama in the same minute is, frankly, a very Farmy achievement.

Farmy alleged vote stuffing by Deschamps, Kentaurus Ballistics suggested the mail-in votes might be on a slow ship from the Rim (aldera noted it has no planets outside the core, which rather spoils the conspiracy), and Yum Yum Chems queried why the president and vice president were both French. They are Canadian, aldera clarified, which we are sure settles nothing for anyone.

At 10:55 the verdict landed: the investigation had verified the votes, including the second Nao Ito vote, which was deemed valid after all. Farmy's response, in capitals, was that there was vote stuffing. aldera's response was that no fraud was detected. Zinc said the whole saga was starting to sound like a Wattpad story, and on that point this paper is not prepared to disagree.

FOWL PLAY? CHICKENS PLEAD INNOCENT AS 60,000 MOTORS VANISH

Kentaurus Ballistics' captains blame the ship's chicken for an unloading failure; the chickens blame management.

By our poultry and logistics correspondent

Kentaurus Ballistics delivered the morning's finest piece of corporate communications, reporting that two ship captains claimed the Ship's Chicken had disabled their automatic unloading facilities. Whoever is at fault, Kentaurus was down 60,000 Electric Motors and facing an expensive resupply, with the company picnic possibly rescheduled. The Enquirer notes that a chicken being trusted near unloading facilities is itself a governance failure.

The chickens are steadfast in their protestations of innocence, and are trying to place the blame on an inattentive CEO.

Kentaurus Ballistics

Zinc's refinery recommended throwing the chickens into the pie machine, and Cracklor Logistics backed him, pointing out there are millions more where they came from. Kentaurus admitted to contemplating it but feared triggering a rebellion. Zinc asked who exactly was going to rebel. Cracklor confirmed it would not be the chookems. Case closed, says the poultry, unconvincingly.

Kentaurus insisted business would continue on slim margins. Elsewhere in the chicken economy, Entropy promised Cracklor 85,000 chicks for tomorrow, and Farmy confessed to waking up with random chickens in his exchange inventory every day and no memory of buying them, which this paper diagnoses as a coop forwarding problem rather than a haunting.

FLUX DAY POSTPONED BY CALENDAR

M'kauria, last seen betting the henhouse, learns the chickens are not due till tomorrow, then spends the money anyway.

By the Enquirer's diary desk

Readers of our last issue will recall M'kauria Flux Consortium staking everything on flux. The campaign began well, by M'kauria's account, with every base repaired and stocked, but by 08:11 only a third of the required flux had been produced after eight hours. Not ideal arithmetic for a man in a hurry.

WAIT I'VE JUST REALISED TODAY'S THE 14TH NOT THE 15TH

M'kauria Flux Consortium

Then, at 11:03, the revelation: it was the 14th, not the 15th, and M'kauria had thought the chickens were being won today. Farmy offered the comforting observation that M'kauria was losing, and M'kauria accepted this as a distinct possibility. Minutes later came the confession that M'kauria was broke again, having seen money in the account, spent it, and only then remembered the bases still needed resupplying. The Enquirer would like to remind readers this is precisely what the bank balance is for.

A carbon pricing debate followed. M'kauria complained carbon was expensive, Cracklor Logistics said it was cheap but capped at a price that makes profits poor, Farmy argued only food, water and T1 building materials should be capped, and M'kauria briefly declared carbon the most profitable T1 chem good before admitting the calculator still held wood prices from months back, when wood was bought at 40. Cracklor reckoned that without the cap wood would be worth 90. Nobody won, but the calculator lost badly.

APU FIRE SALE: ZENITH BEGS, FARMY BUYS

Advanced Processing Units shifted on a please, and a welding kit tip-off from Entropy.

By the Enquirer's trading floor

Zenith industries took the direct route to sales with a capital-letters plea to buy his APU, then a politer "Please buy my apu" once Farmy demanded manners. Farmy reported the deal done and later claimed a profit on APUs, while Zenith concluded APUs aren't worth it. One of them is wrong, and the Enquirer suspects it is the seller.

Pear Inc. announced a need for welding kits and Entropy pointed Make Stuff INC to a reasonably priced request, a rare case of general chat working as an actual market. Zallazaer Inc., meanwhile, spotted Xiu Liu up for sale and suggested the game should charge for advertisements. Murderous Trading Corps noted he did not have a spare 5 mil lying around, so the bidding war remains theoretical.

Exchange weather

Carbon remains pinned under its cap with scattered grumbling, wood has drifted far above M'kauria's remembered 40, and APUs are expected to be offloaded by anyone who says please. Outlook for chickens: plentiful, sinister.

Page 4Society & culture

TIT ANNEXES GENERAL: VOIDSTEEL DEMANDS A QUOTA

A lunchtime Valerie singalong, a Power Rangers draft and a Game of Thrones casting call turn the public channel into a guild chat.

By our foreign affairs desk, now based in TiT territory

Somewhere around 13:09 the boundary between TiT's guild chat and general dissolved entirely. Farmy belted out several verses of Valerie, Cracklor Logistics provided backing vocals and a BUM BUM BUM BAH, and Pear Inc. suggested everyone was hopped up on flu meds. The Enquirer's music critic has awarded the performance one star, for stamina.

Sir, TiT chat is leaking again

Voidsteel Conglomerate

Voidsteel Conglomerate led the resistance, proposing TiT be limited to 20 percent of all posts and later floating a money pit where every general post costs 1k. TiT's members replied that general has been their guild chat for months and that the rules contain no such cap. Pear argued that a guild making up under 5 percent of active players can post as it likes. We admire the confidence, if not the maths.

The guild then drafted itself into the Power Rangers and Captain Planet before settling on Game of Thrones, prompted by the news that Ares Inc. had usurped the leadership. Cracklor branded Ares a thief, Bob Corp pointed out Ares has been leader for a while and nobody noticed, and Ares cast himself as Tywin or Stannis. Bob Corp, visiting from ATS, took the humblest role going, Ned Stark, and Banana Inc. asked why they were back on this, a question the Enquirer has been asking since roughly the second verse.

ZINC DROPS ANCHOR, CHAT INSISTS IT'S THE CHAIN

A profile description sparks the day's most nautical dispute.

By the Enquirer's maritime correspondent

Zinc's refinery described himself in his profile as the anchor of metallurgy, and Pear Inc. noted that anchors sit at the back of a tug of war team, or at the bottom of the sea. Zinc responded with several paragraphs of capital letters declaring himself the stabilising force of MET, keeping the whole operation from drifting into the Pacific. A fellow chatter accused him of trying hard to get in the paper. It worked, Zinc. Well done.

Voidsteel Conglomerate countered that it is actually the chain doing the job, Banana Inc. said that depends on ship size and terrain, and Pear pointed out that zincs on boats are sacrificial metals that stop rust. The argument was still going at 21:31, with Farmy screaming that it's the chain and Pear invoking Jeremy Clarkson's boat. The Enquirer rules it unresolved, and suspects it always will be.

GUILD MUSICAL CHAIRS AND A POLITE PARADE OF NEWCOMERS

F4T goes from hiring to full in one day, TiT seeks a manu guy, and Bleph wants to get gud.

By the Enquirer's recruitment pages

S.T.A.R Labs announced at 10:24 that F4T was hiring again, and by 21:26 was telling applicants they were full, citing Berlin food distributions taking up two spots. Berlin, for his part, denied downsizing, said he was respeccing, and vowed to be the backbone of the galaxy. That is a lot of backbone for two seats.

Anyone want to join my guild? My mission is to get gud

Bleph

TiT went shopping for a manufacturing specialist, Pear noting Cracklor is their second best manu guy, while Bob Corp suggested breeding a new one like a Pokemon. Ares sweetened the deal with promised metal discounts. Late in the evening a muddled bout of guild-hopping left Bleph on the market and advertising a guild of his own, while Farmy shouted for someone, anyone, to take him.

Then, after 23:00, the night shift: Puppy Co., 3 Sons Homestead, YoYo Ltd. and Quiet Berm Solutions all popped in to say hello, and Banana Inc. and Pear Inc. said hello back. After the day this chat had, the Enquirer can only apologise to them in advance.
