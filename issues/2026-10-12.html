Pulse of the Galaxy

Argumentative, well-fed and slightly existential

Galactic Tycoons woke up wanting a fight and Update #31 gave it one. Auctions took up most of the afternoon, from the anti-sniping row to the exec income league table to a sad little stream of players finding out their execs had retired or been sent to the reserve. Executives are clearly what the galaxy cares about this week, and the general feeling is that the big boys can always outbid you anyway.

Underneath that there's a low hum of worry about metal. Skippy noticed Titanium and Platinum slipping before breakfast and said every MET was falling by teatime, while Void LLC begged for mercy on T1 prices. Somewhere in the middle, chat spent a whole morning on death, oxygen and AI slop, which this newspaper reads as a playerbase with a lot of time to fill while it waits on ROI.

SOFT CLOSE

SNIPED! UPDATE #31 SHOOTS DOWN THE LAST-SECOND BIDDERS

The devs patch the auction house, and chat spends the rest of the afternoon arguing over whether that was a crime against buyers or long overdue justice.

By the Enquirer's auction-room correspondent, bidding the minimum

Leki Fuel and Stuff warned of a minute's downtime at 14:04, and by 14:07 Update #31 was live. Nobody needed long to find the headline change. "oh boy anti sniping," said Unicorn Industries, and Zinc's refinery reported the auction house had "slowed down significantly". Medici Mining Conglomerate sighed "Aww, I loved sniping", the closest thing to a eulogy the practice is going to get.

If I get it for less than 75m, wonderful. If I get outbid, I move on with my life

Bob Corp

Banana Inc. led the opposition, arguing that with auto-bidding up to a max price already in the game, a time extension only helps the seller. Dumb agreed, calling antisniping "so lame". Zinc's refinery took the other side and kept at it for well over an hour, bringing out a counterexample, a theory of the time-value of waiting 24 hours on an exec, and finally a lap of honour ("another faulty argument dismantled") after deciding Banana was ignoring him. Banana answered by inviting Zinc to go through every auction sale himself and prove it. We doubt the spreadsheet will ever turn up.

Coss came out of his self-declared hole to tell Banana that online auctions almost always have extended timers, and stressed he was "correcting" rather than disagreeing. Bob Corp, meanwhile, offered the most sensible take of the day and was mostly ignored for it: work out what an exec earns you, set a 15-day ROI, bid that, and if someone beats you, go and find the next one.

Echelon Interstellar Group then walked in and admitted to sniping every auction, mostly to annoy people (see Crime & courts). Sumran Consortium added that he'd spent so much sniping Zinc's execs he might otherwise be at 120B already. So the snipers lost their favourite toy today, and at least one of them had just confessed to his share of the damage.

The Enquirer Says

OUR VERDICT: JUST BID WHAT IT'S WORTH, YOU ANIMALS

The Enquirer has watched two hours of grown tycoons argue about eBay, and our verdict is that Bob Corp was right before most of them had started. Decide what an exec is worth to you, type that number in, and go and do something useful with your afternoon. Turning up at 0.5 seconds to go was never a strategy, it was a hobby, and the devs have now taken it away.

We also admire the honesty of the one sniper who said he does it to upset people. At least the motive was clear. The rest of you calling it price discovery can stop now.

— The Editor

RETIRED (ALLEGEDLY)

EXEC 'RETIRES' AT 44: CHAT SAYS THE RESERVE DID IT

Farmy buys Stefano Grimaldi, finds him already retired, and gets a lesson in what happens to unsold execs.

By the Enquirer's coroner's court reporter

Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th opened the morning by accusing Unicorn Industries of selling him "a lazy mofo" in Stefano Grimaldi, who seemed to have retired already. Quiet Berm Solutions couldn't believe anyone retires at 44, and to be fair neither could we. S.T.A.R Labs offered the cold comfort of "Read before you buy".

he didnt retire. he got retired

Sumran Consortium

Cracklor Logistics. did the detective work and found the exec was listed as retired a day before Farmy bought him, then briefly decided Farmy had killed him. Sumran Consortium put him right: the reserve did it. Farmy asked the devs to add a "retires soon" warning to auctions and then announced he was going back to bed, the only sensible response anyone had all morning.

The case had a sequel at 15:13, when Quiet Berm Solutions reported an exec who "retired" at 33. Lek pointed out he'd most likely been fired, and once Quiet Berm confirmed he'd gone to the fed reserve, Farmy said only that "they blend them up". The Enquirer will not be investigating any further, for the sake of our stomachs.

JOKE, YOUR HONOUR

'BOOSTING CONFIRMED' CRIES ARES OVER 1.1B EXEC SALE

A billion-credit exec changes hands, and a guild leader skips the trial and goes straight to the verdict.

By the Enquirer's suspiciously round numbers desk

Lek reported that Takumi Yamada had sold for "$1.1B or sth.", from Zinc's refinery to someone called Aquaman. Ares Inc. answered within a minute with "BOOSTING CONFIRMED", which this newspaper should point out is not the same as a moderator confirming it. As far as the log shows, nobody official said anything.

Zinc's reply was "iiwii". Lek added a parting shot, "Aquaman: The Art of the deal". For context, chat was busy agreeing that base permit execs regularly go for 700m and up, which makes 1.1B pricey but hardly outrageous. Case unresolved, and we expect it to stay that way.

EXEC RICH LIST: ARES TOPS 725M A DAY, SUMRAN'S 'SLACKERS' SIT IT OUT

The galaxy's executives are earning serious money, and some tycoons have only just noticed.

By the Enquirer's payroll snoop

After the sniping row, chat moved on to comparing how much its execs earn. Ares Inc. put his at around 725 mil a day, while saying he doubts he's spent even 2bil at auction. Echelon Interstellar Group put his at $600M/day, Coss guessed around 200M, Banana Inc. said 100m, and Sumran Consortium simply called his "slackers". That's honest, if not exactly a recommendation for Sumran's recruitment policy.

just saying, I've never crashed a market once

Ares Inc.

Elsewhere, Pūr n Kleen grumbled about paying $39m in recruitment fees for Beatrice Blackwood, and Zinc and Make Stuff INC reassured him she was "fairly decent" and "not horrible". Zinc also reckoned the exec craze is over and prices are coming down. We'll check that against the next 1.1B sale.

Down the table, Ares finished MET level 96 with a 131-day ROI on the next one and promised "MORE QUANT". Sumran, meanwhile, was congratulated on passing 100B and on Trade & Infrastructure Tech reaching 40M prestige. So the snipers have money to burn after all.

METALS SLIDE AS COSS 'JUST COASTS' ON TEN SHIPS OF ORE

T1 metal producers ask for mercy, and the man accused of crashing the price says he's barely trying.

By the Enquirer's foundry floor reporter

Skippy saw Titanium and Platinum slipping at 03:01, and by 16:49 he said "every MET is falling". Coss had already said profits on T1 metals "aren't low enough" and suggested driving prices down further, which drew a "please no 💔" from Void LLC. Bob Corp observed that T1 is still profitable and could go lower. Hardly what Void wanted to hear.

Ares called Coss "the destroyer of markets". Coss said he was "just coasting", that he'd front-loaded inputs when Awaitafar quit, and that he has 10 20MT ships of goodies, around a month of ore. That's quite a lot of goods for a man who's coasting. Zinc's refinery told him his alu ore would run out soon, and the two of them traded "Same bro" for the rest of the afternoon.

Banana Inc. asked anyone and everyone for cheap Tiridium Alloy, and Skippy's only market advice was to stay away from Copper Wire. Neither got an answer, as far as we can see. Meanwhile Make Stuff INC, who said "bye bye 1B" before breakfast, signed off the day at "10B soon". Make of that what you will.

Exchange weather

Cold front over the T1 metals belt, Titanium and Platinum drifting lower, with heavy ore stockpiles expected to keep things grey for about a month. Exec auctions now close more slowly. Snipers should take an umbrella and a lot of patience.
