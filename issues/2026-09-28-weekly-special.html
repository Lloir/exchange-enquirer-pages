STILL UNRESOLVED

IT'S COSS ALL THE WAY DOWN: the saga that ate our whole week

One boast on Thursday became a five-day, four-issue confession spree that ended with a self-declared willess vessel and a verdict from our own editor's desk. Nobody official ever weighed in.

By the Enquirer's retrospective desk

It began, as this paper reported in Two Coss Are Better Than One, with a boast: PvP was the fastest growing guild in the galaxy, Coss said, winking. Ares Inc. called for a boosting check within three minutes, and Coss answered not with a denial but with the line that would define his week: his guild mate, he said, is actually his alt. We ran it UNRESOLVED because no developer or moderator said a word, and readers, reasonably, assumed that would be the end of it.

my guild mate is actually my alt

Coss

It was not the end of it. Saturday's Chemical Warfare Edition gave the alt story a rest while Coss respecced into chemistry and picked a fight with Tato Industries instead, but by Sunday's I'm Sparticus Edition the confession had metastasised. Mittcb declared himself Sparticus. Coss named Cracklor, Ares, Amy and Drunk as his alts in one breath, Medici Mining Conglomerate volunteered as the GP alt with the immortal line "We frequently talk to ourselves because general chat is too empty", and Coss topped the lot by asking for AMY to be banned as yet another alt of his, on the grounds that she takes the rules too seriously. AMY, readers may recall, was the one who had tagged the developers about him in the first place.

By Monday's Coss All The Way Down Edition the list had grown again, now stretching into ATS with Echelon, Lek, Tato and Twisted all named alongside the original four, and a brand new character, Drone inc., had turned up to repeat the exact same two words that started it all: "my alt". Mittcb said he was Sparticus twice, for the avoidance of doubt. By morning Drone inc. was describing itself as no-one, "Just a willess vessel for the ambitions of the GOAT", and this paper's own editor delivered the closest thing the saga ever got to a headline: "It's Coss all the way down."

Four issues, one boast, zero rulings. At no point did a developer, moderator or system message confirm a single alt claim, including Coss's own about himself. What we can confirm is that Coss has now anchored, in some form, every front page we have printed since Friday, which is more coverage than our own spread-the-coverage rule normally allows any one player. We are making an exception for the same reason readers keep reading: it is, whatever else it is, a genuine saga, and it never once resolved itself, which is more honest than most of what gets said in general chat.

THE EDITOR LOOKS BACK: maddest, most overrated, and the one nobody clocked

Five issues in five days. Here is what this desk actually thinks about the week it just printed.

By the Editor, reluctantly

Maddest thing of the week, no contest: the alt saga above, which somehow got sillier every single day it ran and never once slowed down to explain itself. Second place, some distance back, is Cracklor Logistics. managing to be simultaneously deposed from TiT, adopted by SRS, denying he was ever in SRS, and denying he was Cracklor at all, inside about thirty hours.

Most attention for the least payoff: Thursday's ranking war between RNK and TiT, which this paper splashed across a full front page under RANKLED! and which both guilds then simply stopped mentioning, aside from two increasingly deadpan corrections. Compare that to Sunday night, when Echelon Interstellar Group posted three characters, "$100B", in the middle of the alt pandemonium and nobody so much as paused. That is the quiet story of the week: a genuine, unshowy milestone that got a single line of congratulations while chat argued for the fourth day running about who is whose alt.

In the spirit of self-awareness this special edition apparently demands: this paper was reviewed by its own readers on Saturday (mixed, but a crossword was demanded), reviewed against a rival publication on both Sunday and Monday, and asked to run somebody's propaganda, somebody's forged quote or somebody's advert at least five separate times across the week. We said no every time. If there is one throughline to five days of general chat, it might be that everybody has strong opinions about being covered by this paper, right up until we actually cover them.

WHATEVER HAPPENED TO... the Really Official Ranking?

Thursday's front page war between RNK and TiT was too loud to ignore. By the weekend it had simply gone quiet. We investigated. We found nothing, which feels appropriate.

By the Enquirer's league tables desk

As RANKLED! reported, the row started with Pass 4 Denied moaning about his own officers, Sparky of RNK declaring the Supreme Leader the true and only authority, and Cracklor Logistics. founding the Really Official Ranking on the spot, with TiT at number one because, naturally, it says so in the name. Cracklor eventually admitted the whole thing was partly staged for this paper's benefit, which we noted at the time and note again now with no small amount of pride.

Friday's corrections box confirmed the two guilds were, in the paper's words, still at war, with Sparky calling TiT the enemy of the people. Saturday's corrections went further still, clarifying that both guilds still think they are number one. And then, nothing. No mention in Sunday's edition. No mention in Monday's. The Really Official Ranking, as far as this paper's own back issues can show, was never once settled, defended or referenced again after Saturday. It didn't lose. It just ran out of chat oxygen, which may be the single most honest outcome any ranking dispute in this game has ever had.

The Supreme Leader's praises, for what it's worth, remain unprinted to this day.

NO HARD FEELINGS: the Tato-Coss chem grudge match, one week on

She said she'd rather suffer than let him profit. He said challenge accepted. Neither has technically won, and one of them has become exactly what she set out to fight.

By our chemical warfare correspondent

Saturday's Chemical Warfare Edition set the terms: Coss respecced into chemistry to level 60 for about three weeks, and Tato Industries, who lives in the neighbourhood he was moving into, made it her personal mission to keep his chem profits at zero, stockpiles and all. "i don't have any hard feelings, but i have made a game out of making sure you don't make any money off chem," she said, and Coss answered with his usual challenge accepted.

no, i'd rather make it difficult for you, even if i suffer

Tato Industries

By Sunday, the feud had widened into a bigger argument about ATS's chances of ever overtaking GP, with Tato admitting that chasing the top spot was one of the main things keeping her playing at all. Monday's Coss All The Way Down Edition, tracing the whole thing back, confirmed the irony this paper had been circling all week: Tato told chat that the downturns in chem caused by her own defensive stockpiling had turned her, in her words, into a filthy flipper too. She went to war against a market manipulator and came home running the market herself.

No dev has intervened. No winner has been declared. The chem market, last this paper checked, is still a battlefield, and Tato is still, by her own admission, having more fun than usual.

CRACKLOR'S GAP YEAR: deposed, adrift, adopted, denied, all in one week

TiT's most prolific poster spent Sunday begging SRS for shelter and Monday claiming he was never there. We are printing all three versions.

By the Enquirer's migration desk

Sunday's I'm Sparticus Edition caught Farmy McFarm Face The 7th sacking TiT's entire officer corps before lunch, and Cracklor Logistics., freshly stripped of rank, announcing he had been deposed and was now adrift, offering bribes for shelter. SRS's Makuria Inc took him in, on the informal condition that he sponsor a new admin centre wing, because, as Makuria put it, the carpet needs redoing.

its cold out here alone

Cracklor Logistics.

Monday's Coss All The Way Down Edition picked the thread back up: SRS climbed to number 4 on the leaderboard during Cracklor's brief stay, Makuria thanked him warmly for stopping by, and Cracklor tried to come home to TiT, only for Farmy to remove the officers all over again and say no. By the next day, this paper's own reporting shows, the officers had quietly been reinstated regardless.

Then came the denials. Asked whether he'd boosted SRS's prestige during his stay, Cracklor said he hadn't and had only popped in to say hello. Then he said he had never been in SRS at all. Then he said he wasn't even Cracklor. This paper does not choose between the three accounts. We simply note that in a week when half of general chat turned out to be somebody else's alt, Cracklor denying his own name is, if anything, the most on-brand thing that happened all week.

Wanted

Coss

Repeat confession across four consecutive editions, with aggravating self-contradiction

Named himself as the owner of at least eight alts over the course of the week, asked for his own accuser to be banned as one of them, and was never once confirmed, denied or ruled on by anyone official. A committed reoffender in the fullest sense.

Reward: A second copy of Coss. We hear there are several available.

THE QUIET CENTURY: how Echelon banked $100B without anyone noticing

While the rest of general chat argued about alts, one water baron just got on with it.

By the Enquirer's big numbers correspondent

Saturday's Chemical Warfare Edition found Echelon Interstellar Group $2.2B short of a $100B milestone, funding the final stretch with a very cheap sideline in water bought at $13 from other players. Coss briefly threatened to crash the water market just to spoil the party; Echelon worked out the worst case was a delay of about a day.

Sunday it happened. In the middle of the alt-naming frenzy that fills our front page this week, Echelon posted three characters: "$100B". Makuria Inc said congrats. Coss, mid-confession, managed a "Congrats Echelon!!! Well done" before returning to the business of accusing half of TiT and ATS of being himself. Nobody claimed Echelon as an alt, which may be the nicest thing that happened to anybody all week.

It was, by the numbers, the single biggest individual achievement this paper covered this week. It got one line. We are correcting that now.

THE SCIENCE MARKET, FROM GLUT TO GRUDGE

A crowded lab, a no-lowballers sale, and a debate about a prestige nerf that just will not die.

By the Enquirer's laboratory economics desk

Sunday's Research glut: sellers everywhere, buyers nowhere to be seen tracked Pass 4 Denied and Mittcb both trying to unload Quantum Research Data into a market that Lek warned was getting dangerously crowded, with prices sliding from 32k down toward 31k inside a single afternoon. By Monday, in Coss All The Way Down, Pass 4 Denied was still at it, selling under the banner of "No lowballers, I know what I've got", while Farmy McFarm Face The 7th cheerfully admitted to lowballing him anyway and winning.

Running underneath both days was the same argument about admin centres and prestige that Saturday's Admin centre apartheid? first raised: Echelon Interstellar Group's estimate that moving from ATS's admin centre to GR's would cost $280M a day in lost profit, Ares Inc.'s admission that another hundred levels would come in handy even at the top of the table, and, by Sunday and Monday, a fuller post-mortem from Hydrogenica Energies and Tato Industries on whether the prestige nerf had permanently broken ATS's chances of ever catching GP. The consensus by Monday, as Medici Mining Conglomerate put it, was that the real return these days is on the exchange, not the leaderboard.

Three days, one market, one unresolved inequality debate. Science producers, for what it's worth, are advised to sell early and not check the price twice.

Exchange weather

A slow front of alt confessions continues to dominate the outlook, with scattered self-contradiction expected daily through the weekend. Quantum Research Data remains under a glut warning, Coss's chem wall holds steady against Tato's stockpile, and a very quiet high-pressure system of $100B sits, largely unremarked upon, over the ATS water trade.

HAVE COUCH, WILL TRAVEL: the full itinerary of Pear's guild crawl

TiT to F4T to PvP, with a change of plan somewhere along the way. This paper's own predictions did not survive contact with the pear.

By the Enquirer's travel desk

Saturday's Guild crawler Pear goes F4T caught Pear Inc. announcing he was hoboing again, taking offers from Murderous Trading Corps, Farmy and S.T.A.R Labs before landing, loudly, in F4T. Sunday's I'm Sparticus Edition had him already talking about crawling to GP next, with an application to PvP pencilled in for the morning after.

By Monday's Coss All The Way Down, the itinerary had rewritten itself entirely: Pear had gone from F4T straight into PvP, skipping GP altogether, thanking PvP's Potentia - Vis - Proventus for the couch and asking who would take him in next. He even pitched a Chinese-language version of his hobo tour to KOI along the way, with Farmy replying in kind. This paper's own Sunday prediction of a GP stopover, in the interest of full disclosure, did not hold up. We stand by our sourcing and not our forecasting.

Whichever guild is hosting him by the time this prints, the couch, we are reliably told, does not leak. Much.

STILL DECLINING

THIS PAPER, REVIEWED, ALL WEEK LONG

Chat spent five straight days reviewing us, comparing us to the rival press, and trying to write our headlines for us. We said no every time. Here is the full scorecard.

By the Enquirer's navel-gazing correspondent

It started on Thursday, before we had even settled on a name for ourselves this week, with readers spotting a broken link in the archive and Sparky of RNK trying, politely, to insert a line praising the Supreme Leader. Friday brought a full forged chat message, planted by Zinc's refinery and attributed to the real Bob Corp, who denied it in two syllables: "bwah?". We didn't run it. We noted the attempt instead, which is what this column exists for.

They are both entertaining just different flavors. Farmys feels more like business section and holos feels more like personal stories

Entropy

Saturday's readers' panel was blunter still: Holue's Galatic Contruction called this paper a general chat regurgitator, EggInASac called it fun, and Pear Inc. suggested, in language our lawyers made us paraphrase, that everyone should have a beer. A crossword was demanded and duly supplied, though this paper notes for the record that a rival claimant, SL Refining, has been telling chat all week that the crosswords were his idea in the first place. We neither confirm nor deny.

Sunday and Monday turned into a proper newspaper war, with Holue's own GT Weekly under threat of going bi-weekly and Entropy delivering the week's fairest verdict, reused above. One reader tried, twice, to have this paper double-tag a message that turned out to be an advert for his own recruitment scheme; both attempts were noted and neither was run. That makes five separate steering attempts across five issues, by our own count, and five refusals. If nothing else, this week has been an unusually thorough audit of our own spine.

Corrections

Across five issues we called the RNK-TiT ranking row unresolved on four separate occasions. We are now prepared to make a final ruling: it is unresolved, indefinitely, and will remain so unless one of them mentions it again, which as of Monday, neither has.

We described Coss's original guild-mate confession as involving one alt. By Monday the figure stood at roughly eight, depending on who you ask and whether you ask Coss. We regret the undercount, though not especially.
