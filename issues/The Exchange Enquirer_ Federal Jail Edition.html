Page 1Front page

233% in a day. Then jail.

Redshift Endeavors is locked up for boosting after a general chat standoff that TiT bet the farm on, and lost within half an hour.

By the Enquirer's exchange desk

It began, as the great scandals do, with a leaderboard. At half past seven on Sunday evening Sparky of RNK noticed that a guildmate who should have been second on the newspaper's fastest-growers list simply wasn't there. Holue of GR explained the rule: under seven days old, no seven-day growth figure. Case closed, briefly.

Then Sparky spotted something else. TiT's Redshift Endeavors had grown 233% in a single day, from roughly 60 million to around 150 million CV. Holue called it plainly: boosting. He pointed to a pile of cows "mysteriously bought" just as Redshift was selling, cows being one of those goods almost nobody genuinely buys on the exchange.

"Nothing on his exchange in the last 24 hours explains his growth."Holue, standing firm

TiT closed ranks. Ares Inc. insisted it was successful market flipping. Cracklor Logistics argued that the whole economy was on an uptick. SL Refining suggested Holue simply didn't understand it. Farmy McFarm Face The 7th went further than anyone, offering a wager that would echo all night: if Redshift was being boosted by TiT, the devs could ban him permanently.

At 19:53 Farmy took the question to DEV Builder Inc. directly. At 19:59 the answer arrived: at least three people in TiT were boosting Redshift heavily. Farmy's reply was two words long and, to his credit, immediate: "I'm wrong."

By 20:00 Redshift's profile read "Owner is in federal jail." Sparky, who had started the whole thing, admitted he hadn't expected the devs to actually look. Holue, who has spent months being told he was imagining things, allowed himself a "wow what a surprise."

The sting in the tail came fourteen minutes later, when it emerged that the heaviest outside buyer was not in TiT at all. That story, and the jailbreak protest that followed, continues on page 2.

A deal is a deal

Having lost the bet, Farmy spent the next ninety minutes demanding that it be honoured, in capitals. "IT'S THE DEAL I MADE. EVEN IF I HAVE TO GET MYSELF BANNED." Holue observed that nobody was going to make him a martyr. Farmy offered to change his name to Martyr. The devs, sensibly, did not engage.

Also noted: Bob Corp's forensic finding that Redshift's icon isn't even red.

Page 2Crime & courts

Coss: "This one is 100% on me"

The biggest buyer steps forward, TiT claims victim status, and a guild petitions to be arrested.

If TiT hoped the story would stop at Redshift, it did not reckon with Coss. At 20:13, freshly arrived in TiT after a turbulent week, he laid out his own part without being asked. His habit, he explained, was to invite guildmates to send him a contract at whatever price worked for them and accept it blind. He had done exactly that with Redshift.

"I was buying nanotubes from him for 2x current market on 3 separate occasions. It was roughly 22k units."Coss, in general chat

He told the devs to action him if needed and said he would take any punishment necessary, while restating that he is firmly against boosting beyond reasonable guild or friend discounts. General chat, being general chat, responded by proposing that his punishment be to grow, then to restart fully self-sufficient, and finally that he rename himself Moss.

Cracklor Logistics offered the defence's closing argument: four boosters and five semi-boosters, most of them outside TiT, with Coss only a minor player. TiT, he said, was the victim of targeting and "anti TiT propaganda." Pear Inc. summed up the room's reaction: "Ok so tit is the victim." Sumran Consortium noted it was the second time a TiT member had been banned for boosting, and suggested disbanding the guild, winky face included.

Tato Industries offered the evening's most honest take on the rules themselves: boosting is kept a deliberate grey area, because clear lines would just be gamed. Everyone does a bit of it. The trick is not to get caught.

The great jailbreak (into jail)

Protest

What followed will be studied by future historians of the galaxy as the first recorded protest in which the demand was to be arrested. From about 21:25, TiT members took turns asking DEV Builder Inc. to ban them. "WE HUMBLY REQUEST TO BE BANNED," read one petition. Ares Inc. wanted a ban so he could stop playing. Farmy wanted a ban on principle.

At 21:34 Pear Inc. broke the news: Farmy was in jail, reportedly for spam. Cracklor declared a riot over unfair dismissal, arguing the offence was historical. Hashtags bloomed: #FREEFARMY, #FREEFLIP, #FREECARLY. Farmy was released at 21:40, at which point Lek reported that Flip Co had been jailed in his place. Cracklor, still free, complained that people on Discord were mocking him for not being banned.

Drunkenduo's Ruthless Dividend, who admitted to being part of the boosting, left TiT for a guild that the chat understood to be a one-man prison. Pear Inc. later joined it too, and asked if anyone wanted to visit him in jail. Frugal Factories raised the jurisprudential question of the night: if you join voluntarily, is it still jail?

The confession nobody asked for

At 22:10, hunting for a ban of his own, Cracklor announced that he had washed the chicken market several times by passing trades back and forth to lift the average price, and that he once jumped 2.7 billion CV in a day against a usual 400 million. Drunkenduo said he helped. Cracklor said he was surprised it never got flagged.

The Enquirer notes, without judgement, that a confession typed in public chat to get yourself banned is still a confession typed in public chat. Whether it was true, or simply the loudest bid yet for a jail cell, is a matter for the devs.

Exile economics

Cracklor, briefly guildless, discovered the no-guild surprise at the worst moment: his 800kt ship landed at an exchange warehouse capped at 3kt, with roughly 10Mt more to unload later. "I have made a grave, grave error," he reported, from outside the admin centre he had helped fund with 700m in donations the day before.

Pear Inc. later posted a warehouse reading 5,537,789 used against a 3,000t cap.

Page 3Business

Coss takes the crown at PvP

48 hours after joining TiT, the galaxy's most discussed trader finds himself leading his own guild, with a science plan to match.

Coss logged in on Monday night to learn that he had been removed from his old guild and was now officially the leader of a new one. According to Cracklor, the motivation behind his promotion was simply that ATS would be furious. StarkyDog Enterprise congratulated him on finding the fee. Coss called it a steep expense and said he now needed to recruit.

"No single person will feel like I've abused them, the entire galaxy can!"Coss, outlining the PvP mission

His first order of business is ambitious: flood the galaxy with T4 science, run the chain at net neutral, and sell at breakeven, flattening everyone along the way. AMY of RAS summarised it as Coss declaring war on science itself. Asked by Ares how long until PvP overtakes GR, StarkyDog guessed six or seven days; Coss said there would be little donating and mostly warehouse.

By Tuesday afternoon he was already weary. Three new planets had produced four complaining DMs. ManuCo suggested going fully vertically integrated so nobody could object, and the two exchanged a warm farewell after their time together in ATS.

Page 4Society & culture

Pear's odyssey ends in a fruit basket

One man's journey from TiT loyalist to prison volunteer to a guild that is renaming itself after produce.

Few citizens of the galaxy had a longer 24 hours than Pear Inc. He opened by demanding that DEV Devine Intervention ban TiT, pledged an exposé on how TiT cheats, and announced that he needed to protect his clean-boy reputation. Then he joined the jail guild, then declared "I MISS TIT" the next morning, then went looking for a guild that could break him out of prison.

By mid-afternoon he had landed. "I AM IN MY NEW HOME," he wrote, briefly alarming Makuria Inc of SRS, who saw the notification out of context. Asked by Flip Co how things were going, Pear reported that his guildmates were all renaming themselves after fruit, that they planned to ask Builder to rename the guild the fruit basket, and that Rearden Steel was considering Banana Steel. AMY, meanwhile, has made it clear there is no escape from her basement.

He is also, by his own account, now beta-testing Farmy's side game and filing his feedback in the only format he knows: capital letters, a request for structural frames, and a complaint that he cannot partially load cargo.

Old soldiers of the internet

Late on Sunday the protest gave way to something gentler: veterans remembering older games. Farmy, formerly of BoB and Reikoku in EVE Online, was outraged to be mistaken for a Goonswarm member. Entropy called Goonswarm the TiT of EVE, and nobody disagreed. The two traded favourite Goon-era songs, Farmy's "Little Bees" singalong was roundly declared awesome and made before AI, and Entropy lamented that CCP had fallen off.

Cracklor recalled leading an ICC raid in World of Warcraft and singing along on Vent during long boss runs. Farmy summed up the mood shift: from angry old men to reliving their teen years in one evening.

After midnight the talk turned to Factorio, with TiT's shared base described as one giant build around two spaghetti messes, and then to Dwarf Fortress, which CCCP of GR called the greatest of all time. Cracklor once ran a fortress on a water clock. For Armok.

The bread corner

Just before midnight, general chat briefly became a baking forum. SL Refining has given up shop bread because it always went stale before he finished it, and now bakes his own on a silicone mat that gives a perfect bottom crust. It was a basic one from Walmart.

Cracklor's honey loaf smelled like honey on toast while it baked. His comfort food is honey on toasted rye; SL prefers cheese, then butter, then everything bagels. Cracklor's advice: bagels are just a stiffer dough, boiled then baked. Stale bread becomes bread pudding.

Dinner that night: mince on brods.

Heard around the galaxy

Bob Corp introduced Farmy to "Heads Will Roll," which Farmy had been trying to find for ages and then played on repeat. Farmy reported that Destiny 2 is back from the dead. Cracklor promoted Ore and Orbit, a rival space trading game, roughly once every twenty minutes for the entire night, noting on each occasion that it gets updates.

New arrivals

Three newcomers appeared at 04:04 and were offered a place in GTB within a minute. Their main question was whether guild friends are nice or abusive. Told abuse was allowed if consensual, sobeantuh rock sorting llc replied that he had to apply now.
