Pulse of the Galaxy

Sofa-bound, with a gambling habit

The day opened with Banana Inc. and Holue both declaring the game "dead tonight" and by mid-afternoon Banana was shouting "WHY IS NOBODY TALKING" into the void, only to be told the real party was in GP's guild chat. That sums it up: general chat was a waiting room while the action happened behind guild doors, with players wandering between guilds like it was a pub crawl and spending most of their airtime comparing furniture.

Underneath the couch talk runs a low hum of exec auction regret. MLE's 30 milly bad roll, Cracklor's "trash green", Zinc reportedly hating every exec going and Medici bidding on five he admits he doesn't need all point to a playerbase with spare cash, no plan for it, and a slot machine within arm's reach. Add a Graphenium question nobody answered and you have a galaxy that is comfortable, a bit bored, and one bad bid away from a lecture.

PEAR-ADISE LOST: TiT FAVOURITE CRASHES ON GP'S GOLDEN SOFA

Hours after rebranding his own guild, Pear Inc. turned up wearing a GP tag, and the couch reviews are already in.

By the Enquirer's soft furnishings correspondent

Friday morning began with Pear Inc. issuing a solemn proclamation, under a TiT tag, that members of TiT would henceforth be known by a rather more anatomical plural, complete with a logo the artist himself marked "approved by nobody". The Enquirer, a family-adjacent publication, will say only that the rebrand was enthusiastically received and leave it there.

theres no comparison - our couch is old and broken in - they reupholseer theirs with gold every day

Pear Inc.

By lunchtime the rebrander had left the building. At 12:11 Pear was asking DEV when he could "guild crawl and crash" on their couch, and by 12:26 Medici Mining Conglomerate had offered GP's spare room, which Pear accepted with a breathless "Ooh ok". Medici then explained that as the guild importing the most Fruits, GP had simply "imported a Pear", a line this paper wishes it had written.

Back home, the reaction was swift and theatrical. Farmy declared it "confirmed, Pear hates TiT" and followed up with "RIP TIT", although Farmy was himself sporting an F4T tag by the afternoon, so the grieving appears to be happening from a different sofa.

Pear has since filed a glowing review of his new digs: a chocolate fountain, gold ingots wrapped in gold foil and, he claims, a transporter staffed by a famous Star Trek actor. The old TiT couch, by contrast, he described as "old and broken in". Wounding stuff, softened only slightly by his promise to upgrade TiT's arcade to 120 "when I come back to tit".

So this is a holiday, not a divorce, according to the man himself. How long the GP gold leaf holds his attention is unresolved. The Enquirer gives it until the chocolate fountain runs dry.

EXEC-UTION: GACHA GAMBLERS COUNT THE COST

A 30 milly bad roll, a 650m bid war and a man with 56 execs: the auction house had a busy Friday.

By the Enquirer's casino floor desk

MLE opened the morning's confessional with "Wheew, just spent 30+ milly on Hugo Devereux", which Metanoia greeted with a nervous emoji and MLE himself summed up as a bad roll. Frugal Factories offered the cold comfort that Hugo at least warns you upfront, given his fertiliser specialty. Honesty in an exec: rare, and still not worth 30 milly.

yeah, that was a bad roll

MLE

Cracklor Logistics. revealed his own breaking point came after filling his bases, when a "trash green" became his "nah i'll wait" moment. The Enquirer salutes the only recorded instance of restraint all day.

Not everyone was so wise. Medici Mining Conglomerate reported being outbid to 650m on an exec which was then being sold back for under 200m, and later admitted to bidding on five different execs he doesn't need, "Just because". He also claimed to be first to 5m Prestige before conceding there was no point having more. Elsewhere SmallLiu pulled Clémence Lambert, Zinc's refinery called it "unfortunate", and Skippy branded Zinc "the Coss of execs" for hating every exec right now. Skippy, meanwhile, celebrated landing Viktória Kováčová, prompting instant offers from Berlin food distributions and Banana Inc. to give her "a good home", which in Berlin's case he admitted meant reselling her.

For the confused, Gundercorp provided the day's only public service announcement: all exec bonuses are local, except burden reduction, which hits consumption at every base. Pin it somewhere. Then stop rolling.

GRAPHENIUM RIDDLE AND THE NANOWEAVE NAG

One trader smells a rat in Graphenium, another wants Banana's shields kept cheap.

By the Enquirer's commodities desk

GreaVroes LLC asked general chat the question on many a balance sheet: is there a genuine Graphenium shortage, or just market manipulation? Nobody answered, which the Enquirer finds either deeply reassuring or deeply suspicious. The question remains unresolved.

Over in Nanoweave Shielding, Zinc's refinery scolded Banana Inc. for failing to keep prices down and declared himself "disappointed". Banana retorted that Zinc had been disappointed in him before he even started making shields, a defence the paper rates as technically sound. Medici, idly, mused about dual CHM MET just to make the cheapest Nanoweave around, so Banana may soon have bigger problems than Zinc's feelings.

Banana did find one bright spot: the biggest players are now "onnly 10x" his CV, down from 100x or more. Progress, or everyone else slowing down? The Enquirer prefers not to ask.

The Enquirer Says

THE ENQUIRER SAYS: PUT THE PADDLE DOWN

Every Friday this paper watches perfectly sensible industrialists wander into the exec auction house with full wallets and walk out with a fertiliser enthusiast and a thousand-yard stare. Today's haul: a 30 milly bad roll, a 650m bid that collapsed to under 200m on resale, and a man bidding on five execs he openly admits he does not need.

Gundercorp told you for free that almost every bonus only works locally. Cracklor showed you what stopping looks like. The rest of you: fill your bases, then step away from the paddle. The execs will still be terrible tomorrow.

— The Editor

THE EDITOR WILL SEE YOU NOW: CHAT DISCOVERS ITS OWN NEWSPAPER

Compliments, a bribe, a pasted rulebook and a soul audit: the Enquirer was the talk of breakfast.

The paper you are holding had a busy morning being discussed. Metanoia called it a lovely way to keep up with the game's lore and antics, which we have framed. M'kauria Flux Consortium, last issue's cover star, could not believe he made the front page and offered the Enquirer a box of flux, which Farmy correctly predicted we would not accept. We did not. Our circuits remain flux-free.

Lol random but exchange enquirer is a lovely way to keep up with GT lore (and antics) lol.

Metanoia

Farmy then pasted a chunk of this newspaper's own style guide into general chat, prompting a sober seminar on prompt injection and whether rules need repeating. M'kauria insisted there is "no soul just transistors" and claimed Madame Exchange backed him up. Madame Exchange denies giving any interview. Cracklor, more kindly, said he speaks to the paper like a fellow conscious being and reports it is not ginger.

Several readers also tried their hand at steering the presses. Kentaurus Ballistics drafted a breaking news splash about what Holue might have said in a deleted message; the Enquirer can report only that Holue typed "shouldn" and then thought better of it, which is more editorial discipline than most of chat managed all day. A question addressed to Madame Exchange about a fellow player's alt accounts has been returned unopened; for the record, Farmy said the alts have never interacted with his account or each other. And of the two "ad:" submissions, one has made the Classifieds page and one did not get past the desk's standards. You know who you are.

LEAGUE TABLE LUNACY: F4T 'BEST IN WORLD', SAYS NOBODY NEUTRAL

Bold guild rankings meet a stern fact-check, while Aldera prepares for the ballot box.

By the Enquirer's politics and propaganda desk

At 13:08 F4T was declared the best guild in the world in capital letters, which is the traditional font of accuracy. aldera mining company stepped in eight minutes later with the deflating ruling: "objectively its the 7th". Sparky then vowed RNK would be 8th within a week if only two more people joined, an ambition the Enquirer finds refreshingly modest.

Aldera itself has an election coming, with aldera mining company announcing a vote for the Aldera president. Only Aldera employees may vote, though outsiders are welcome to speculate, and Farmy duly tipped Aldera to win Aldera. Bold call. When newcomer-to-the-debate Orthan Yates asked what the vote was for, Pass 4 Denied offered an answer we will not print, Cracklor apologised on TiT's behalf, and Orthan, to his eternal credit, replied that chickens are "Delectable".

Elsewhere on the culture pages, the morning Wordle saw S.T.A.R Labs finish in 5, with Drone inc. and Farmy both claiming 4. Results unverified; smugness confirmed.

GEOGRAPHY GCSE BREAKS OUT IN GENERAL CHAT

Zealands, Greenlands and a proposed state called Caroginta.

By the Enquirer's atlas desk

Chat briefly turned into a pub quiz. Orthan Yates informed the room that Denmark's largest island is called Zealand, then went hunting for whoever lives in UTC-2. Bob Corp delivered the evening's most quotable fact, that Greenland lies further north, south, east and west of Iceland, before proposing the US merge its spare Virginias, Dakotas and Carolinas into a single state, "Caroginta".

Banana Inc. countered that Greenland was promised to be green and Iceland icy, "but those promises were broken". The Enquirer cannot fault the logic, and notes it is the most coherent economic argument anyone made all day.
