Pulse of the Galaxy

Cluckish, with retirement jitters

General chat spent Tuesday mostly in a good mood and mostly talking about poultry. The chicken buy-up gave everyone something to cheer or complain about, and the complaints were theatrical rather than bitter: one long lament for the lost 960$ chicken, a few sellers puzzled to find their stock gone, and a growing suspicion, voiced by more than one player, that chickens might be the smartest investment in the galaxy.

Under the jokes there was a little worry about the long game. Execs announcing their retirement had players doing sums on their staff and joking about mass walkouts. There were grumbles about clunky interfaces and a companion app drawing the wrong icons, and one player publicly asking the paper to count the days since the last update. Nobody is in revolt. But a galaxy that sends coded messages asking when the auction is coming is clearly waiting for something.

SOLD OUT

FOWL PLAY! DAY OF THE CHICKENS RETURNS AS FLOCK IS HOOVERED UP

The galaxy's poultry is bought out at 820$ a bird, Oseberg mourns the lost 960$ chicken, and by lunchtime there is apparently nothing left to buy.

By the Enquirer's poultry desk

It is happening again. By 08:11 Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th had declared that "day of the chickens #2 is upon us", and for once the Enquirer cannot accuse a TiT member of exaggerating. Cracklor Logistics. spent the morning buying up the exchange's chickens, and the sellers lined up to say thank you. YoYo Ltd. thanked him outright for clearing their stock, and Cracklor, never one to pass on a pun, said he likes to "cluck up the economy sometimes".

The "funny" guild brings nothing but sadness and depression, robbing others of their chickens.

Oseberg inc.

At 07:48 he posted the receipts: a chickens line reading 12,200,000, 820.00$ and 10,004,000,000$. The Enquirer will not pretend to know exactly how those numbers fit together. We will only say that any chicken order with a ten-billion figure attached deserves its own weather system. Zinc's refinery then announced he had "just crashed chickens", twice, and S.T.A.R Labs replied "No you didnt" and "Nuh uh". As rebuttals go, that is the most convincing one this paper has seen all year.

Not everyone was cheering. Oseberg inc. published a lament that would make a Victorian widow blush, calling Cracklor a "chicken THIEF" for taking away the noble 960$ chicken and leaving behind "a measly 820$ chicken". Oseberg also accused him of gloating and of puffing up a CV built "on the tears of chickens and others". Medici Mining Conglomerate's response: "Sounds like capitalism. I love it." Cracklor's own verdict: "the best form of capitalism, fake money built on other peoples work". Nobody in this galaxy is ever going to be short of self-awareness.

By 11:09 Cracklor declared "i can buy no more chickens", and Skippy shouted "Lies!". Cracklor later credited Coss with reaching "in deep" to get the last chickens out for him, and at 12:05 announced 4.4b of growth for the day, his best yet. Meanwhile Medici proudly reported owning 69 chickens, which we assume is a portfolio choice and not a joke.

The after-shocks went on all afternoon. Skye Blu Foods noticed someone had been buying chickens again, Bob Corp mourned a sold chicken, and Pass 4 Denied looked over the trade history and suggested chickens might be "the single most profitable good in the game". Mittcb's investment advice: hoard them and wait for "a crazy genius" to buy the lot. "Might be weeks, might be months." Based on today, it might be Tuesday.

NOTED, IGNORED

PAPER DECLINES ITS DAILY ORDERS, AGAIN

Readers try to hand the Enquirer a to-do list. The Enquirer files it with the chickens.

By the Enquirer's complaints department

At 06:08 S.T.A.R Labs posted a paragraph in our house style, solemnly reminding readers that this paper does not take dictation. We are touched. We are also clear that it came from S.T.A.R Labs and not from the Enquirer, which writes its own pompous paragraphs, thank you.

Moments later Pass 4 Denied tested the theory by addressing "@enquirer" and "The Editor" with a list of demands: set the mood to "update when", add a daily count of days since the last update, and include suggestions for the next one. They even said please, which we appreciated. Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th predicted "it won't", and Farmy was right. We will grant that the mood of the galaxy does get a box on this page. It got there by being reported, not ordered.

At 07:43 Cracklor Logistics. asked the paper to "note" his chickens. That one we did, but only because the chickens were news. A cheeky suggestion in chat is still a suggestion, and the newsroom will not be shifting from that position.

EXECS GIVE NOTICE: STAFF START BOOKING THEIR RETIREMENT PARTIES

The first execs announce they are leaving, and chat realises its whole workforce is mortal.

By the Enquirer's human resources correspondent

Lek brought the news at 09:49: the first execs have announced when they plan to retire. They are not his, he was quick to add, but they belong to someone, and one gave two days' real-life notice while another gave four. Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th asked if anyone had actually agreed to that contract, which is the right question and one that will never get answered.

cant wait for my 50 execs to all abandon me within a week

Zinc's refinery

The magic number seems to be 59, the age Lek said they announce, with the actual retirement coming at "60, 61 or even later, who knows". Zinc's refinery responded the way any responsible employer would and looked forward to his 50 execs all walking out together. Medici Mining Conglomerate wished for execs who stay 80 days longer, and Zinc went further and asked for immortal execs and no limit on numbers, so he can "amass an army". Skippy suggested whoever loses a fight gives up an exec, and Zinc rightly identified that as Pokemon.

Pūr n Kleen suggested turning unwanted execs into Meat, or failing that Fertilizer. The Enquirer reports this purely as an economic proposal and will be locking its own office door tonight. On the more humane side, SL Refining is putting Kenta Watanabe through university so people will buy him. Zinc said "no one is gonna buy that lil bro", and SL hit back by pointing at Zinc's large pile of unsold stock. Ouch.

Drunkenduo's Ruthless Dividend, meanwhile, presented Austin Reeves as the worst HR recruit ever. Murderous Trading Corps offered to "handle him". We are choosing to believe that means a fertile planet in the belt.

ONE BASE, ELEVEN RESEARCH, ZERO REGRETS

A stalled CV, a firm no to expanding, and a newcomer who actually listens to advice.

By the Enquirer's small business desk

Void LLC opened the day in the small hours complaining that his CV had been "stagant for like 2 days". When Zinc's refinery suggested building more bases, he answered "NO" in capitals, then asked for anything he could sell for a red price, "unless it means getting a new base". Planet Nibbler did a quick audit and found the problem. The Enquirer would also like to know why you'd stop at eleven research before buying a second base.

you have 11 research but not a second base

Planet Nibbler

There was better news at the other end of the day. At 23:03 newcomer Ty Corp asked whether to terraform more building slots or upgrade the buildings he had. Quiet Berm Solutions gave a sensible answer: build out what you have, grow your earnings, and only expand once you have operating margin to spare. Ty Corp said thanks. A beginner asking a question, getting a helpful answer and saying thank you is so rare in this paper that we checked twice.

Elsewhere, YoYo Ltd. recommended spending stars on ships before more bases arrive. Holue's Galatic Contruction thanked the devs for Nyxara Kordan, an uncommon exec that Echelon Interstellar Group called "pretty good for a green". Mittcb, at 00:19, advised anyone under 2 billion CV to message Sumran for cheap produce. We could not tell if that was a public service or a prank, and the Sumran Consortium was not available for comment.

MYSTERY CIPHER CRACKED: IT WAS ABOUT AUCTIONS ALL ALONG

Metal Rabbit drops a coded message into chat, the Enquirer's codebreakers crack it over a cup of tea, and by evening everyone is grumbling about search buttons.

By the Enquirer's Bletchley desk

At 15:59 Metal Rabbit posted, without explanation: "Dsvm ziv dv tvggrmt zfxgrlm rm gsv tznv?" Pear Inc. answered "yup" straight away, which suggests either a fellow codebreaker or someone who replies yup to everything. Swap each letter for its mirror in the alphabet and the message reads: "When are we getting auction in the game?" It took real effort to ask the most-asked question in the galaxy in a way nobody could read.

"If nobody bids, the Federal Reserve acquires them at the starting price." - WUT

Pūr n Kleen

Earlier, Cracklor Logistics. had been quipping about a feature that is "actually gonna happen", unlike the auction house. By the late evening, though, chat was talking about auction searches as if there were something to search. SL Refining described clicking through traits, and Zaay said bluntly that the "auction search function is bad".

Pevilot SA asked DEV Leki Fuel and Stuff whether a fusion kit bonus really speeds up Bioxene extraction, since that was not what the tooltip said. Zaay said it didn't, and that the search was simply showing him the wrong results. Cracklor called the interface clunky and unintuitive with too many clicks. Pūr n Kleen found the line that really caught everyone's eye: if nobody bids, the Federal Reserve buys the item at the starting price. His full review was one word, "WUT".

BlueStar had a tech complaint of his own: GT companion was drawing company HQs with the guild HQ icon. Lek admitted the last update might have changed how icons display, "oops", and BlueStar called it "literally unusable". A man who knows how to overreact properly. The Enquirer salutes him.

Exchange weather

Morning flocks moving fast across the exchange, with chickens heading sharply up into one buyer's warehouse and settling at 820$. A cold front for anyone waiting for 960$. In the afternoon Ares Inc. warns of rising tiri prices, and Fizzy Pop Inc advises bracing for impact.

LAMEST, ALLEGEDLY

LOGO WARS: SL REFINING DECLARES HIMSELF GOATED, CHAT DISAGREES

A row about company logos turns into dueling 'Official Assessments', an unpaid conmats claim, and a haggle over tiri.

By the Enquirer's style and branding editor

It began nicely enough, with SL Refining saying Lek has the second coolest logo in the game. When Zenith industries asked whose was best, SL Refining named himself. This newspaper has been to plenty of awards dinners and has never seen a winner hand himself the trophy before the envelope is opened.

Ima increase my prices

Ares Inc.

The vote went against him. Zenith said "Yeah no". Nightsman nominated Farmy's logo. Zinc's refinery called SL the lamest, "your not the best, but youre the lamest", which hurts the most because it's so specific. A side debate over whether Zenith should be "Zi", "IzI" or "|z|" ended with Zenith settling it with "That's a fire z". Nobody won. Everybody typed a Z.

Then came the assessments. Pear Inc. described SL Refining as "Suspiciously Lubricated", and SL Refining then posted an "Official Pear Assessment" that rated his own competence "annoyingly high" and named him a NANOSHIELD BARON. Zinc answered with his own, much less flattering assessment, parts of which this family newspaper has chosen not to print. SL's reply, that it's nice to ask people how they're doing, was the most wholesome thing said all afternoon.

The business side was just as tangled. Pear Inc. said SL owed money for conmats he had ordered and not paid for. SL replied that he had since bought the conmats, and Pear seemed satisfied, so we will consider the matter closed. SL then challenged Zinc to beat his tiri price from Ares. Zinc replied that he did not want to step on Ares's territory, SL said it wasn't his territory, and Zinc guessed the price was 5600. SL refused to confirm. At 16:35 Ares Inc. stepped in with five words that ended the negotiation.

COSS NORRIS: CHAT INVENTS A LEGEND

A man who wasn't even in the conversation gets a full wrestling entrance and a book of tall tales.

By the Enquirer's mythology correspondent

Coss did not post in general chat today, but he still had the busiest afternoon in the galaxy. The story that he had fished out the last chickens set off a wrestling commentary nobody asked for: "RKO OUT OF NOWHERE" from Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th, "MACHO MAN COSSY SAVAGE" from Cracklor Logistics., and Farmy's "Stone Cold Steve Cluckstein", which this paper considers the pun of the day.

TiTCoss has spent so long on gas planets he can breathe argon

Useless Inc

From there it turned into Chuck Norris jokes. Medici Mining Conglomerate said that when Coss Norris plays dodgeball, the balls dodge him, and that he can upgrade an SR ship to LR by throwing it across the galaxy. Cracklor said that when Coss lands on Trore 2 the wet gets him, and when he mines Kentaurus 2 he pushes the planet away instead of lifting the ore. Useless Inc. said Coss can breathe argon and builds his buildings by intimidating the ore, which this paper finds completely believable.

Useless Inc. had started the day at 03:52 saying he might be the dumbest person on the planet, and didn't explain. Turning up hours later with the best joke of the set is a strong recovery, and the Enquirer approves.

GP'S CHARM OFFENSIVE: SKIPPY RECRUITS EVERYONE WHO STANDS STILL

A dev, a guild master, a solo player and a whole debate about infinity.

By the Enquirer's transfer window desk

Skippy of GP had one job today and stuck to it. First he invited ATS Lek to GP (Cracklor immediately counter-offered TiT). Then he worked on Cracklor himself with "Come be uncool like me then, we need you". Then he tried R&D's Pevilot SA, who turned him down because he has no overhead problem. Pevilot did lose a point by admitting he was under 100 levels, and Skippy told him to start expanding.

you fool. a infinite number + a finite number still makes infinite

Infinite Margin

Skippy said GP had taken "one of GR guys", and Medici told chat to check Discord for GP's new recruitment poster. That brought GR's Infinite Margin into the conversation, asking how he had become "best recruiter ever". Cracklor offered to distract GR by throwing finite margins. Infinite Margin pointed out that infinity plus a finite number is still infinite. GreaVroes LLC added that the foolishness was infinite too and only made them stronger. Cracklor said he would ascend. The Enquirer stopped taking notes at this point.

Elsewhere in the transfer market, Pevilot SA asked whether Awaitafar had left or been kicked from a TiT guild, and Skippy said he was in GP. Unicorn Industries noticed that TiT Trade & Infrastructure Tech had lost some people, and Farmy mentioned a jump from "rank 14 to rank 3". If that rank move is as clear to you as it is to us, you are doing better than we are.

The Enquirer Says

THE ENQUIRER SAYS: STOP SELLING YOUR CHICKENS, YOU FOOLS

Twice now this galaxy has watched its whole chicken supply get bought in a morning, and twice now the sellers have said thank you. We understand the appeal of a quick sale. But when Pass 4 Denied looks at the trade history and calls chickens possibly the most profitable good in the game, and Sumran Consortium suddenly decides it needs to keep chickens at high prices, it may be time to wonder who's really the clever one in these trades.

Mittcb's advice is the only sensible take we heard all day. Hoard them, be patient, and wait for the next crazy genius. Skye Blu Foods is now asking whether it has to start making chickens. Yes, Skye Blu. Yes, you do. Oseberg's 960$ chicken is gone, but the next one is waiting to be built.

— The Editor
