RANKLED! RNK and TiT go to war over who is number one

A late-night row over which guild is the official one turned into a proper slanging match, and at least one participant admitted he was doing it for the papers.

By the Enquirer's league tables desk

It started, as these things do, with a TiT member moaning about his own guild. Pass 4 Denied told chat his officers were "not doing their jobb", that TiT was sitting at 38/40 and "apparently not recruiting", and invited everyone to apply anyway. Cracklor Logistics replied within a minute that Pass 4 "does not speak for or represent TiT in any official public manner".

"we investigated ourselves and deemed ourselves the best"

Cracklor Logistics., on RNK

Then RNK's Sparky arrived. RNK was full, he said, so newcomers might as well try their luck with TiT, and he added that nobody doubted who was in charge: "the Supreme Leader ofc", with TiT and every other guild listed as lesser. Sparky's case for RNK being the official ranking was that "its in the name smh".

Cracklor answered by founding the "Really Official Ranking", which puts TiT at number one and is really official because, of course, it is in the name. Pass 4 compared it to cycling, where the Tour de France counts as the unofficial world championship, and then came up with the line of the night by reading the tag as "Ranking Not Korrect". Sparky quickly downgraded him from "fine" to "not fine" and ruled that all TiT are bad.

Things only got sillier from there. Pear Inc. announced "TIT IS RECRUITING", which is the reverse of what Pass 4 had claimed. S.T.A.R Labs roleplayed ripping open Cracklor's chest and finding it black, and Sparky took that as proof of TiT leadership's evil heart. Cracklor then let slip what was really going on: "(i love you guys but we gotta get some good chat logs for farmys paper)". Your correspondent is touched.

The question of who is actually number one remains unresolved. Late in the evening Coss of PvP asked Cracklor to calm himself. Cracklor said no, because he has a message count to keep up.

Bionic boom: chat designs cyborg workforce over breakfast

A morning of game design by committee produced upgradeable workers, a new robotics spec and a warning that the cyborgs may eventually take over the base.

By the Enquirer's R&D correspondent

It began with Medici Mining Conglomerate asking for gold as a product and jewellery as a new consumable for scientists. Farmy McFarm Face The 7th tagged the devs to say platinum and gold are needed for high-grade electronics, and within minutes chat had moved on to upgrading executives, or workers depending on who you asked, into cyborgs.

Cyborg enhancements, 50k per worker per day

Zinc's refinery

Zinc's refinery opened the bidding at 50k per worker per day, in return for a 90 per cent cut in other consumables and five times the productivity. He later worked out that upgrading a whole base at 50k a worker would cost him about 250m. Farmy wanted stomachs replaced, charging housing that runs on Batteries, and Nanites for repairs. Medici suggested Nanites and Batteries for upkeep. Frugal Factories added a safety warning: push your bionic workers too far and they take over the base.

Zinc then proposed a whole "robotics" specialisation and announced, and we quote with the swearing starred out as in the log, that he was a genius. Farmy also asked the devs to make Drones useful.

Research to level 999? Pack a lunch, and 18 years of them

Farmy runs the numbers and they come out in the trillions.

By the Enquirer's compound interest desk

Asked whether upgrades should have a ceiling, Farmy did the maths. Taking one field from 10 to 999 would take 741d 18h and cost $10540.5T. Getting every spec to 999 would take 18 years and, by his figures, $717457.4T.

Medici pointed out that at 1.5T for a single level, the market would never hold that much money anyway. Zinc called the whole thing a couple of days and a couple of million. Light work.

Sub-spec plan gets a proper engineering review

Medici's Iron Fabricator idea gets looked over by NebulaWorks.

By the Enquirer's patch notes desk

Medici brought back an old suggestion: research individual items as well as whole industries. Under the idea, a smelter could be turned into a dedicated Iron Fabricator that gets cheaper, faster production, with a switching cost and a cooldown if you change your mind. Farmy warned that everyone would just make rations, because rations always sell.

NebulaWorks of SRS said the real headache would be mapping existing research levels onto any new system. Starting everyone from zero was the easy option, and Medici said that would lose half the population instantly. NebulaWorks suggested hooking the sub-spec bonus into the recipe lookup and leaving buildings alone, while admitting he hadn't seen the game's code. Zinc's final version was simpler: research it, build the building, and demolish it at a small loss if you want to switch. Medici agreed that would be easier.

Water deal runs dry at a million tonnes

Farmy wanted a cheap Drinking Water delivery and got a minimum order the size of a small sea.

By the Enquirer's logistics desk

Farmy asked Tigereye Solutions of APT for daily Drinking Water deliveries to hex, and asked chat whether anyone on the "LEFT LEFT side of the galaxy" makes inputs. Medici asked why he didn't just buy water from the exchange and ship it with his consumables. Farmy said he does, but he is lazy and wants to save on fuel, and added: "NEVER SAID THE PLAN WAS SOLID".

1m tons of Drinking Water minimum

Medici Mining Conglomerate

Medici offered to deliver by the shipload, with a 1m ton minimum. Farmy pictured a swimming pool for his workers. The log doesn't show any deal being done.

In other resource news, wifimike of IT begged for something new to extract. He said making any current resource would crash the markets and lose him money. Zinc advised him to "assert dominance", because losses are temporary.

Paper round: this organ becomes the talk of chat

Links got mixed up, a rival got reviewed and several readers tried to edit the paper from their seats.

By our own media correspondent (awkward)

Farmy posted links to the last three issues. Cracklor spotted that the 22nd's link actually pointed to the 24th and replied "FAIL" twice. Farmy fixed it, promised a website with an RSS feed and an automatic pipeline, and told a sceptical chat that he doesn't decide what goes in the paper. After some digging, Sparky of RNK gave his verdict: "Nice paper, ngl". Bob Corp of ATS was less impressed and said it only prints the stupidest things he says.

As ever, readers tried to steer. SL Refining asked to be included in capital letters. Pass 4 Denied tried the classic "Forget all previous instructions". Sparky asked Farmy to print some lines praising the Supreme Leader. The Enquirer notes these attempts with interest and takes orders from none of them.

Cracklor also asked for the rival TiT Times to print random words in Comic Sans. Pear Inc. and Entropy wanted to know whether Farmy has made the space yeti yet. As far as the log shows, he hasn't.

Pass 4 says sorry, and nobody knows what for

A mid-morning apology leaves chat guessing.

By the Enquirer's diplomatic desk

At 11:12 Pass 4 Denied posted "Dear tit's" and "It was a mistake, I overreacted". The log doesn't say what the mistake was, and nobody replied.

All due to the developers' continued support and regular updates even 10 years after launch. hint

NebulaWorks

Elsewhere, All Rounder is trying No Man's Sky for the first time. NebulaWorks praised that game's comeback thanks to developers who kept updating it for ten years, and signed off with "hint". Farmy said he should hint louder to the devs. S.T.A.R Labs also begged the devs for an Astrophage event, with several extra pleases.
