Pulse of the Galaxy

Reflective, slightly exhausted, oddly fond of the whole mess

The final stretch of the chat archive finds a galaxy that has stopped being new and started being, simply, a place people live. The anniversary numbers below speak for themselves, but the mood in general chat these last four months has been less triumphant than companionable: the same arguments about boosting, the same jokes about chickens, the same tycoons turning up every single day to complain about the same things in the same voices.

This paper considers that, on the whole, the highest compliment a tabloid can pay its own beat.

ANDROMEDA TURNS ONE

A full year of general chat, finally totted up in one place

By the whole of the Enquirer's newsroom

On 23 September the developers marked Andromeda's first birthday with a full accounting of the year, and this paper is delighted to report the numbers are, if anything, an undersell of how much nonsense actually happened along the way.

Exactly one year ago, on 23 September 2025, the first companies landed in Andromeda.

Leki Fuel and Stuff

Nineteen thousand and thirty companies were founded over the year. Between them they produced two hundred and forty billion units of everything from rations to research data, sent five point three five million ships flying a combined sixty one million light years, and hauled one hundred and forty two billion tonnes of cargo in the process. Twenty one point nine trillion credits changed hands on the exchange. Two hundred and two guilds were founded to argue about how to spend it.

And, central to this publication's entire reason for existing, two point two three million messages were typed into chat across the year, every one of them a potential headline this paper simply did not have the pages to run.

The developers closed the announcement with characteristic warmth, and this paper, not usually given to sincerity, finds it has nothing to add except agreement.

HR DEPARTMENT OPENS, GALAXY ASKS ABOUT BREEDING RIGHTS

Executives finally arrive, eighteen months after the devs first teased them

Late August brought one of the year's most anticipated features, when Update #27 introduced Executives: hireable specialists bringing per-base bonuses, headhunting, and an entire talent market this paper expects to be writing about for years to come.

We don't plan to add breeding

Builder Inc.

True to form, the first question out of general chat's mouth was not about talent pools or burden reduction perks, but whether executives could be bred. The developers' answer was mercifully swift and unambiguous.

An auction house followed a fortnight later, and chat spent the rest of the quarter arguing about poaching rules, headhunting odds, and whether 'Thorough Search' was worth the doubled cost. Some things, this paper notes, never change.

BOOSTING: THE SCANDAL THAT NEVER ACTUALLY LEFT

A year on from the copper ore affair, chat is still catching tycoons inflating their own numbers

By the Enquirer's crime desk

Readers of Part I will recall Lek Manufacturing's copper-trading scheme as the founding year's defining scandal. This paper is sorry to report the crime never actually went away, it simply changed its name to 'boosting' and kept happening roughly once a quarter ever since.

Space Inc extreme boosting noticed via growth trends. 22x his daily growth or so.

Coss

The most recent case broke on 3 October, when a TiT member spotted Space Inc's growth trends spiking in a manner no legitimate production line could explain, and reported it rather than let it slide.

The developers' response, when it came, was brisk and entirely satisfying: a warning, a fine, and confirmation that the gains in question ran into the billions.

A separate, older case surfaced the same week concerning a company called Redshift Endeavors, with the developers confirming the overwhelming majority of its value had arrived by the same route. Whether these are two isolated incidents or evidence of a wider habit this paper cannot say, though it notes the pattern with some interest.

What is certain is that Andromeda's economy, a year in, still runs on the honour system rather more than its tycoons might like to admit, and that honour system is still being tested on a semi-regular basis.

Wanted

Space Inc

Boosting in the third degree, growth-trend edition

Caught after a guildmate noticed his daily growth running at roughly twenty two times anything production alone could explain. Fined on confirmation; exact sum disputed between the developers and the tycoon who reported him.

Reward: A fine, a warning, and this paper's sincere hope he spends the credits on something more interesting next time

THIS PAPER NAMES ITS CHATTER OF THE YEAR

The Enquirer runs its own numbers on general chat, independent of anything the developers published

By the Enquirer's statistics desk

The developers' anniversary figures cover the whole galaxy. This paper, with narrower and frankly more important interests, has spent the week running its own count across every line of general chat recorded since launch, and can now declare its findings.

Across the year, one company account outtalked the entire rest of the galaxy combined: Cucklor Logistics, who has personally generated a wall-shaking share of every message sent in general chat since September 2025. No single rival comes within half that figure. This paper does not know what Cucklor Logistics produces for a living, but it is clearly not short of things to say about it.

At the guild level, TiT dominates general chat by a similarly wide margin over its nearest rivals, F4T and GP, which this paper notes tracks rather neatly with TiT also supplying the year's loudest guild-pride moment, the Space Inc boosting report, and no small number of the names in this issue generally.

Chat was, if anything, busier at the end of the year than the beginning: October 2026 alone produced more messages than any single month since launch, proof that a galaxy does not get quieter simply because it gets older.
