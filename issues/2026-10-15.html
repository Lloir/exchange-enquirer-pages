Pulse of the Galaxy

Spreadsheet-sore by morning, tribal by bedtime

Thursday's chat came in two clean halves. The morning was a seminar on the state of the economy nobody had booked: construction kits too dear, Fine Rations either too cheap or exactly the same as ever, Honeycaps falling despite demand, Drinking Water wobbling, and dev3l inc. declaring the whole economy broken. There's a low, grumbling suspicion out there that prices are being dragged down to production cost, and that only the fully self-sufficient will be laughing at the end.

Then the evening shift clocked on and forgot about money entirely in favour of who leads RNK, where The Void Cartel went, and whether a faction war was brewing. It wasn't, really, but aldera mining company had its gloves on anyway. In between, the newcomers kept asking sensible questions and getting genuinely helpful answers, which the Enquirer finds faintly suspicious but will allow.

NOT THE SAME

ONE BASE TO RULE THEM ALL? CHAT SAYS 'WE ARE NOT THE SAME'

A breezy hot take about base counts collides head first with a 3,500M CV spreadsheet, and the Enquirer was there with popcorn.

By the Enquirer's growth-envy desk

It began, as all the best rows do, with the words 'Unpopular opinion'. At 09:23 dev3l inc. announced that '1 base is enough for everything', with a little :P on the end in case anyone thought it was a manifesto. Zenith industries responded with the closest thing chat has to a formal rebuttal: 'wtf'.

We are not the same.

Frugal Factories

Frugal Factories then reached for the receipts, as Frugal Factories does. dev3l inc., it was pointed out, had grown to 137M CV since starting on 11 June, while Frugal Factories, starting in August, had hit 3,500M CV. The Enquirer has checked its maths and can confirm that one of those numbers is bigger.

dev3l inc. was not having it, arguing that gameplay is identical whatever your size: everybody still produces, delivers and sells, 'Repeat. Nothing esle.' It asked, with a straight face, whether Frugal Factories had 'some secret or special gameplay options'. The reply came back in four words that will be embroidered on cushions across the galaxy: 'We are not the same.'

Cracklor Logistics. supplied the commentary box, calling it 'the eternal fight for mediocrity' and noting that 'i do less then you but we are equal' is a hard line to hold. In fairness to dev3l inc., the earlier claim that the economy is drifting towards production costs, rewarding those with fully self-sufficient chains, was a serious point. It just got rather buried under the CV comparison, which is what happens when you bring a philosophy to a spreadsheet fight. No winner was declared, though the scoreboard rather declared itself.

The Enquirer Says

THE ENQUIRER SAYS: PRODUCE, DELIVER, SELL, BRAG

dev3l inc. is not wrong that every tycoon, big or small, is doing the same three things on a loop. Where the argument comes unstuck is the bit where some people do those three things twenty-five times as profitably. Simple loop, wildly different results: that's not a broken game, that's a game.

Still, this paper admires anyone who walks into general chat with a :P and leaves with their opinion intact. One base is enough for everything, apparently, including an argument.

— The Editor

Page 3Business

FINE RATIONS: SPIKE, SLUMP OR SAME AS EVER?

The breakfast table becomes a bond market as chat argues over whether food is getting dearer, cheaper or just sitting there looking smug.

By our food and beverage correspondent

Berlin food distributions opened the morning complaining that inflation had 'hit us hard', with Rations and Fine Rations up a lot since starting. Medici Mining Conglomerate, who said 'I'm pretty sure' with the confidence of a man who has seen charts, countered that Fine Rations had been near enough the same price for months.

The food situation on the exchange is definitely trending oddly.

Oate's cheesy oats

Frugal Factories offered a counterpoint: Fine Rations at 161 on 18 August, around 115 through August, and around 97 in October, with executive consumption buffs keeping the gap relevant. Medici waved off the August spike as 'only a few days' and insisted it was looking at six to eight months, not one. Both parties left convinced. The Enquirer, which has never eaten a Fine Ration, remains neutral and peckish.

Elsewhere on the menu, Daniels Inc. reminisced about Rations crashing below 30 in its early days, GreaVroes LLC reckoned Rations are 'just sitting at fed price', and Oate's cheesy oats noted that input costs keep rising and some crops are making a loss while prices still fall, citing Honeycaps. Food. declared Rations 'chill' but warned that Drinking Water keeps dipping, and later bagged a shipment of Ale and Vegetables $2 under the next seller. A small victory, but in this market you take them.

Food. also asked why construction kits are so expensive now. Neatonia's diagnosis was brutal and probably correct: nobody wants to make them. Food.'s only comfort was that tools are cheaper. Every cloud.

NEWCOMER'S WELDING EMPIRE GETS FREE CONSULTANCY

KGHM S.A. asks one question about T2 factories and receives a full business plan, a supplier offer and a shocking revelation about planet chat.

By the Enquirer's small business desk

KGHM S.A. was grumbling at 09:19 that T2 factories cost twice as much to build, when upgrading T1 gave 1.5x the output for the same money. Zenith industries and Cracklor Logistics. both pointed out that higher tiers pay off with more research, and Cracklor added that spamming T1 early on is cheap and consistent. A rare outbreak of chat agreeing with itself.

oh... people use planets chat's?

KGHM S.A.

Frugal Factories recommended T1 bases near the Exchange Station at KGHM's 1.3M CV, and tipped Copper Wire and Truss as the top earners. Handily, KGHM already makes both from welding plants, reporting margins two to three times better buying metals in than smelting its own. The newcomer's worry that 'the money maker will stop at some point' marks it out as wiser than most veterans.

Cracklor Logistics. offered Aluminium ore and oxygen cheap and on site at Aetheria 8, saying it usually gives new players minimum price or thereabouts. KGHM politely declined, already buying low from its guild and flying out to Daedalus 6 for cheap welding kits. Frugal Factories found the Daedalus run 'weird' for a new player; KGHM explained Truss leaves spare weight for ore on the way, which Frugal conceded was 'a new one'.

The real scoop came at the end, when Frugal suggested posting WTB messages on the Aetheria 8 channel and KGHM discovered that people actually use planet chats. Pear Inc. confirmed it had once tried to get Bohemian Rhapsody going in one. Results of that experiment were not disclosed, and the Enquirer fears the worst.

Exchange weather

Fine Rations holding steady around 97, or wildly swinging, depending which forecaster you trust. Drinking Water expected to keep dipping, construction kits stay scorching as nobody can be bothered to make them, and a cool front of cheaper tools moves in from the east.

WHO IS THE SUPREME LEADER? RNK IDENTITY CRISIS GRIPS CHAT

A loyalty notice, a disbanded profile and a mining company spoiling for a fight make for a tense evening.

By our faction affairs correspondent

At 19:56 Sparky informed chat that 'you have lost affinity with rnk', which was ominous enough to bring Entropy out asking who exactly this 'supreme leader' was. Entropy then appointed itself supreme leader at 20:32, a coronation conducted entirely without witnesses. Yum Yum Chems nominated StarkyDog Enterprise for the job instead.

how can you be king with me, if i've never heard of you before?

Sparky

StarkyDog Enterprise turned up at 21:09 with a recruitment pitch worthy of a cult: join RNK Official Company Ranking 'if you want to see the true light'. Yum Yum Chems replied 'NO' and 'TIT'S BETTER', in capitals, which is how you know it's a firmly held belief.

Sparky, who said a WhatsApp had summoned it after hearing chat was 'talking sheet', then asked why it featured in Yum Yum Chems' profile. Yum Yum explained the guild had said 'disbanded'. Sparky's 'oh' at 21:12 is the sound of a feud deflating, though not before the line of the night. Sparky then went to bed, leaving the throne as vacant as ever.

Meanwhile Yum Yum Chems asked what happened to The Void Cartel. Entropy said pretty much all of them 'fell of the grid', and aldera mining company offered the alternative theory that Farmy ate them. Entropy demanded to know how Farmy could. Farmy has not responded to the charge, and the Enquirer stresses that no cartels have been confirmed digested.

CONCLUSIONS REACHED, LOST, ECHOED

A late-night philosophy seminar and a confession from the least mining-focused mining company in the galaxy.

By the Enquirer's words-that-rhyme desk

Just after midnight, M'kauria Flux Consortium announced 'I have come to the conclusion' and then simply stopped. Planet Nibbler suggested that perhaps coming to the conclusion was the conclusion, and M'kauria graciously agreed. Planet Nibbler followed up with a tongue twister about inclusions, occlusions and disillusion, which a user called '...' repeated word for word, orion constructs summed up as 'explosion', and the Enquirer's subs are still lying down.

M'kauria resurfaced at 08:44 to announce 'I lost', prompting a startled 'WHAT?' from Zinc's refinery. Lost what, we are not told. The conclusion, possibly.

In the evening, aldera mining company, having earlier promised that no lore is planned unless something big happens, issued a formal statement that despite its name no mining had been done within the company for the last 100 years. It also warned it has experience with faction wars, which the paper suspects is what it has been doing instead of mining.

Further down the chat, Make Stuff INC and Brutal ultimate company wow traded rival 'challenge (IMPOSSIBLE)' jabs, one about being a killjoy and the other about crying over how the market works. It was declared a draw on the grounds that the Enquirer would like to keep its printing licence.
