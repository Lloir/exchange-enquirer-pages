Pulse of the Galaxy

Giddy, broke, and proud of it

This is the first of three bumper issues putting the entire chat archive on record, from the day Andromeda opened to the day this retrospective went to print. Part I covers the founding months, and the overwhelming mood was pure delight at being confused together. Nobody understood the research cost formula, several tycoons could not tell you what maths was, and all of it was somehow more fun for that.

Early settlers spent as much time arguing about spreadsheets as they did building anything, which this paper considers a very healthy sign for a new economy.

CONFIRMED

AND ON DAY ONE, THERE WAS IRON

Andromeda's first tycoons arrive confused, broke, and thrilled about it

By the Enquirer's founding desk

Andromeda opened its books on 23 September 2025, and within days general chat had settled into its permanent state: half the galaxy asking how to build a second base, the other half arguing about research cost formulas nobody could actually solve.

Capitalism, Ho!

Linnet Co.

By early October the tone was set. One settler, surveying the new economy's incentives, declared it best summed up as 'Capitalism, Ho!' This paper has checked the historical record and can confirm nothing has improved on that assessment since.

The developers marked September's first month with gold medals for growth, production and transport, to general delight and the obligatory grumbling that one company had somehow won nearly everything at once. Early days for sour grapes, but the galaxy was already finding its feet.

A full year on, this paper can report the joke has not aged a day: tycoons are still broke, still confused by the research tree, and still thrilled about it.

FIRST BLOOD: MULTI-ACCOUNTER SHOWN THE DOOR

TAiLS becomes Andromeda's first recorded casualty of the funnelling rules

Barely a month into the galaxy's existence, the developers delivered their first piece of law enforcement: a multi-accounter going by TAiLS had their extra companies quietly removed after a report from a concerned tycoon.

Well I removed your multi-accounts, I guess you don't want to play anymore?

Builder Inc.

The developer's own verdict was dry enough to print as the charge sheet itself.

Chat's reaction split neatly down the middle, between tycoons relieved the rules had teeth and tycoons wondering aloud how anyone thought funnelling through a shared IP address would go unnoticed. One bystander called the whole affair 'honestly mad nice of the devs', which this paper takes to mean the sentence was lenient by Andromeda standards.

How it unfolded

  1. Update #1, 3 OctAuto-scroll and mobile notification fixes; the first sign anyone was listening to the bug reports.
  2. Update #5, 16 OctStarting speed bonus extended from 10 to 14 days, easing new tycoons off the training wheels more gently.
  3. Update #6, 17 OctBuilding maintenance overhaul: repairs can now go above 100 per cent, and degradation slowed by a third.
  4. Update #7, 24 OctHQ and Guild Project costs rebalanced upward 10 to 30 per cent across the board, to a chorus of groaning.
  5. Update #8, 29 OctCompany icons upgraded from 5x5 to 7x7 pixels. The developer admits clicking Next fifty times and changing two pixels.
  6. Update #17, 31 DecReferral programme launched and an entire wing of building and ship costs quietly halved, closing out the founding year.

IRON AGE GIVES WAY TO THE AGE OF IRON ORE

The Exchange's first real price swing arrives, and everyone has an opinion

By the second week of October the galaxy's first genuine market cycle was under way, as smelters raced to turn a glut of ore into finished iron and promptly discovered they had made the same bet as everyone else.

The era of cheap :Iron is over, the era of cheap :Iron Ore has arrived...

Zhate Logistics

One trader on the exchange floor summed up the turn in terms this paper is happy to canonise as the founding economic law of Andromeda.

The Federal Reserve, meanwhile, spent its first months absorbing whatever the galaxy over-produced and quietly capping what it under-produced, a role one tycoon nearly tested to destruction by pricing up a buyout of the entire polyethylene reserve for a mere seven million credits. It did not, in the end, refresh in his favour.

FED RAISES FARM PRICES, FARMERS UNMOVED

On 1 October the Federal Reserve made its first intervention on record, lifting the floor under agricultural goods to keep early farming viable while the galaxy was still finding its feet. The official notice was characteristically blunt about it.

We increased value of Agriculture products to make profit more fair while we are in early stages of the galaxy.

Leki Fuel and Stuff

Nobody in chat seemed especially grateful, which this paper understands is simply how farmers are.

ALLEGEDLY NOT A LEGITIMATE TRADE

THE GREAT COPPER SWINDLE: A COMPANY CAUGHT TRADING WITH ITSELF

Lek Manufacturing's medal campaign unravels in spectacular, self-inflicted fashion

By the Enquirer's crime desk

The founding year's biggest scandal broke on 26 December, when the developers announced they had caught Lek Manufacturing running what this paper can only describe as the galaxy's first insider trade on itself.

Lek Manufacturing did not trade materials for profit.

Builder Inc.

The scheme, in the developers' own account, involved shuttling millions of credits' worth of copper ore back and forth with a teammate purely to drag the average price above twice its normal value, since resource extraction medals reward higher-value materials more generously than they probably should.

The verdict was delivered with the sort of weary patience this paper associates with a parent confiscating fireworks. All the trades were reverted, and Lek Manufacturing was, in the developers' words, politely told not to do it again.

Chat largely agreed it was a creative scheme let down by an obvious tell: nobody else was trading copper that enthusiastically with themselves.

Wanted

Lek Manufacturing

Medal fraud in the first degree, copper division

Wanted for trading with himself hard enough to double the average price of copper ore, purely to inflate his own resource extraction medal. Trades reverted; dignity, less easily refunded.

Reward: The eternal suspicion of every other smelter in Andromeda
