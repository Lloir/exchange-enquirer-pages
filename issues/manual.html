WALL STREET, SECTOR 7: Coss opens PvP to billionaire market movers

A 25B entry fee, 30 to 45 day plays and a plan for half the galaxy's poly: PvP spells it out, and TiT asks for the version for five-year-olds.

By the Enquirer's exchange desk

At 17:51 Coss asked general chat whether anyone had worked out the best return on investment for the endgame, and answered his own question with a recruitment pitch for PvP. His argument: you can't manipulate markets in a traditional guild because 'everyone will start crying', so why not join a guild built for it.

You can't market manip in your traditional guilds, everyone will start crying.

Coss

Asked by Ares Inc. what the minimum was, Coss said 'likely around the 25B mark' in liquid cash, and that these would be long plays of 30 to 45 days rather than quick one or two day flips. He named Kryon as the current target, said he was adding another 6000 levels of refineries that day to get close to 50 per cent of daily poly production, and reckoned quad could be driven below 1700 and still turn a profit at current Tess and Kryon prices. Give it a month, he said, and PvP should have about 150B of liquid capital.

Not everyone was sold. Farmy McFarm Face The 7th told him to 'just get on with it' and then asked him to explain the move like he was five. Coss said that if you couldn't already follow it there was no hope, and Farmy concluded 'so there's no move'. Pevilot SA wondered aloud whether TiT was for people who aren't smart enough for complex tactics, and decided it seemed like his place.

V.O.C. then spelled it out plainly: buy Kryon below the listed price, sell at the listed price, slowly eat the market until smaller players have to leave and you have pricing power. Coss confirmed 'This is correct' and said he had been walking the price down over the last couple of days. V.O.C. offered the old investing warning that the market can stay irrational longer than you can stay solvent. Coss said he liked him.

Later that evening Coss said he was on pace to donate around 10B a week and asked someone to tell him to stop. Murderous Trading Corps volunteered to take the money instead. The offer is, at press time, unaccepted.

UNPROVEN

Fun Police raid GTB over seven-day growth chart

S.T.A.R Labs spots a crowded top ten and reaches for the handcuffs; nobody from GTB answers.

By our man in the charge room

At 21:52 S.T.A.R Labs of F4T said they suspected Galactic Tycoons with the Boys of boosting, pointing out that five of the top ten on the seven-day CV percentage chart were GTB companies. No moderator or system message followed, and nobody from GTB replied, so for now it's one player's suspicion and a leaderboard screenshot.

Last guy who did that went to jail

Zenith industries

Ares Inc. was quick to take the defence, saying S.T.A.R Labs 'hates it if anyone is having fun'. S.T.A.R Labs didn't argue with that, and declared in capitals that they were the fun police.

Earlier in the day the subject had come up in a lighter way. Zinc's refinery offered to boost Zenith industries to '150m in 1 week trust', with SL Refining adding that the starter bonus would make it a 'double boosh'. Zenith turned it down with the day's best bit of legal advice.

The GTB matter is unresolved. The Enquirer will keep an eye on the chart, as will the Fun Police, clearly.

Wanted

Pear Inc.

Aggravated guild-hopping and loitering with intent to find a couch

Spent the day announcing in chat that he was after 'a couch for the night' or a cardboard box. Declared RNK the last stop of his guild crawl part 2 and promised to come home to TiT, then by morning was asking ATS to accept his application because he needed an admin centre. Sumran Consortium said they couldn't. Whereabouts unresolved.

Reward: One slightly used cardboard box and a guild invite (TiT has said #banned, so probably not theirs)

Pie row: who 'fixed' it and who just dumped it?

Pie climbs back towards normal and chat can't agree whether that's a disaster or a correction.

By the Enquirer's baking correspondent

Pie was on the move all day. At 16:10 华润万家 offered to buy Pass 4 Denied's freshly bought Pie for $390, and SL Refining promptly tried to sell them a 1.5m stack at 500. Pass 4 Denied had earlier spotted 华润万家's 15m stack 'flashing' and called it a 'Big fail'.

I have to sell it before the other 2 notice 😂

Medici Mining Conglomerate

By 22:00 Make Stuff INC wanted to know, loudly, who had messed up Pie. Entropy said it was simply back to a normal-ish price after being very cheap for a couple of weeks, and Quiet Berm Solutions agreed that the price history showed it was still cheaper than usual. Entropy put normal at 460 to 470 and reckoned around 510 would be fair now that inputs are up about 20 per cent.

Coss said he had fixed it. Just after midnight Entropy thanked both him and Medici Mining Conglomerate for a steadier supply. Medici said he hadn't done anything clever, he'd just dumped what he produced, and happened to land right on Entropy's idea of fair value.

45 bases, six ships and one very tired miner

The great permit debate: expand forever, or consolidate? Chat splits down the middle.

By our logistics desk, currently on hold with a shipyard

aldera mining company announced they have 45 bases, '5th in the world' (Ares Inc. corrected that to 6th), and only six ships. aldera is aiming for the top 10 in metallurgy and said they'll keep building bases 'Till morale improves'. At 19:36 they reported having only two bases left to resupply.

admin cap is a lie that doesnt exist

Sumran Consortium

Coss argued that expanded bases are terrible value next to consolidated operations. Ares Inc., on 26 bases with 52 permits used, called consolidation 'a noob trap', and Sumran Consortium weighed in with 27 bases and 54 permits. Sumran accused Coss of trying to gaslight people into stopping expansion. Coss replied that the further they expand, the easier it'll be for him to tank their markets.

Overnight it turned into an overhead-measuring contest: Coss about 400 per cent net, Sumran 543, Medici 570, Entropy 310. Sumran's rule of thumb is overhead equal to your production bonus plus 100, and Ares agreed. Entropy went away, did the maths, and decided they need five more admin centre levels and two more electronics levels before expanding.

Exchange weather

Warm spells over Pie, settling somewhere between 460 and 510 depending on who you ask. A cold front is slowly pushing Kryon down from the PvP side. Scattered Argon gusts, with one buyer paying $13 a unit more, and Farmy reports rations fed prices as 'ouch'.

Page 5Society & culture

Fresh from PrUn, newcomer is told: reset, and don't panic

Willow Enterprises asks where to get construction materials and gets a crash course from the veterans.

By the Enquirer's welcome wagon

Late on the 28th Willow Enterprises asked how to get construction materials for their base. Medici Mining Conglomerate explained: buy them on the exchange and ship them over, or pick up a contract on the planet. Willow said the game looked a lot like PrUn, where they'd spent about three years.

Imho, this game is better than PrUn.

Thundercorp

Thundercorp said it was 'faster paced' and, in their opinion, better. They suggested Willow learn the ropes and then reset, since the first 24 hours and first 14 days get a big boost. The record, according to Thundercorp, is about 700m CV in 14 days, held by Gregor GmbH. They also pointed Willow to gt-companion.com, the spreadsheet everyone optimises with.

Holue's Galatic Contruction quickly advised Willow not to measure themselves against 700m. Ares Inc. offered some realistic benchmarks: 100m in two weeks is decent, and 300 to 500m is excellent. Makuria Inc. added that it helps to have people on your planet to trade with.

Thumbs versus mice: the great mobile debate

Is phone play a horror show or a 2G triumph? Chat can't agree, but everyone hates tapping that tiny arrow.

By our technology correspondent, typing with one thumb

Makuria Inc. reported trying to play on their phone in the morning and called it a horrible experience. Holue's Galatic Contruction and SL Refining agreed. Aranei of KUR disagreed, saying the game is in the top 10 per cent of browser games for mobile and works fine on a 2G connection. Makuria's reply: that just shows other browser games are bad on mobile.

Plus, it's nice that a 2G network is sufficient for this game.

Aranei

The base planner took the most criticism. Pass 4 Denied posted a link to suggestions for it. EggInASac wants to be able to change how far the up arrow jumps. Frugal Factories and Neatonia mentioned shift-click on PC, and NebulaWorks said a long press gives steps of five on mobile Chrome. Medici said long press hasn't worked for them on Brave, and summed up the job of taking a level 200 warehouse to 400 as clicking up 200 times.

Fizzy Pop Inc called on the mobile-only crowd to unite. Pūr n Kleen would settle for ordering a whole wishlist in one go.

Enquirer learns it has a letters page (and a grieving founder)

Chat works out how to send ads to the paper, and the fate of Farmys 1 to 6 comes up again.

By the Enquirer's navel-gazing desk

Farmy McFarm Face The 7th explained to Makuria Inc. that anything starting with 'AD:' should turn into an advert, and said he was working on a 'Dear Editor' version for letters. Makuria worked out the point: real letters and ads, 'not just random AI generated ones'. Adverts from people promoting themselves appear on the Classifieds page as usual. Farmy also said Holue's Galatic Contruction now had a permanent advert spot. Holue answered '???' and wondered who was going to pay for it.

i would never

Sparky

When Zenith industries asked what happened to Farmys 1 to 6, Farmy said they died in accidents after going to the supreme leader for help, and that some people say Sparky was involved. Nobody could confirm it. Drone inc. had a different version: the early Farmys were recycled in a sulfuric cesspit until a semi-competent one came out.

Elsewhere, the devs named a planet Zenith 1, and Zenith industries was thrilled. Li4KinG said they now had to populate all four planets in the system. Zenith said they were 'too smol'.
