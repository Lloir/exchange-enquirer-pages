CHAT DEMANDS WEEKLY PAPER, PAPER RESPONDS WITH THIS

With just 30 messages to work from, a reader suggests the Enquirer cuts back. The Enquirer thanks them for the front page.

By the Enquirer's Department of Self-Preservation

At 06:24 on a sleepy Saturday, Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th looked out over the galaxy's general chat and reached a verdict this newspaper finds deeply hurtful and, worse, hard to argue with: the paper should go weekly because there is not enough content.

time to move the newspaper to once weekly.....not enough content

Farmy Mc Farm Face the 9th

YoYo Ltd. backed the motion within a minute, offering that weekly is good enough, which is the kind of glowing endorsement we will be putting on the masthead.

The Enquirer would point out, with dignity intact, that the complaint itself filled a gap in an otherwise contentless morning, and that by complaining about the lack of news Farmy has personally become the news. We call that a self-solving problem.

In fairness to the critics, the day's other big stories include a chicken trade union and a man who wants exactly one turnip. We rest our case, though we are not entirely sure which side of it.

ALLEGEDLY RESOLVED

SHIP'S CHICKEN UNIONISES: CRISIS 'HOPEFULLY' OVER

Kentaurus Ballistics briefs the galaxy on a poultry labour dispute nobody knew was happening.

By our industrial relations (and coop) correspondent

Seemingly in answer to Farmy's content drought, Kentaurus Ballistics stepped forward at 06:32 with the scoop of the day: the Ship's Chicken has unionised and formalised its arrangements with the company. Kentaurus reported good forecasts for the AGM despite setbacks, with one or two executive overseers credited with hitting targets.

if it helps any, it appears the Ship's Chicken has unionized, and has formalized their arrangements with the company.

Kentaurus Ballistics

Farmy's response was to ask what Kentaurus was smoking and whether there was any going spare, which this paper considers a review, if not a good one.

Undeterred, Kentaurus doubled down, explaining that it had been touch and go whether the Ship's Chickens would survive as an entity at all, with frightened crew, missed production targets and unsettled senior management. The yearly party on Reverie 2 is apparently highly anticipated. The Enquirer hopes the union negotiated an open bar.

We cannot independently confirm any of this, chiefly because it concerns a chicken. Still, it is more industrial action than most companies in this galaxy manage in a month.

Corrections

Last issue we reported that Thea Wagner was worth a billion, or twenty, depending on who you asked. We are happy to clarify that both figures remain exactly as accurate as they were.

The Enquirer also wishes to make clear that last issue contained enough content. We counted.

MAKE STUFF INC SITTING ON A MOUNTAIN OF WELDING KITS

Four million kits, three million sofas, no takers on record.

By the Enquirer's warehouse desk

Make Stuff INC turned up at 05:46 announcing a stockpile of 4,070,949 Welding Kits and 3,090,120 Furniture, and asked who wanted them. At the time of going to print nobody had said they did. Seven million items and the galaxy shrugged.

I need more money to research

Make Stuff INC

meowco asked the reasonable question of whether Make Stuff was getting out of Welding Kits. The reply was a single, slightly baffled "no?", so the kits will keep coming and nobody has worked out where they go yet.

Later on Make Stuff INC admitted it needs more money for research. The Enquirer has a suggestion involving roughly seven million things in a warehouse, but we are only a newspaper.

MIDNIGHT CONCERT: VIVALDI, CHOPIN AND A BIT OF BLITZBALL

The galaxy's tycoons trade ore for overtures in the small hours.

By our arts and leisure correspondent, in a cravat

The day opened on a cultured note, with Elegant Mining Co. declaring at 00:05 that Vivaldi is great. M4xKo nominated Winter as the peak season, and Medici Mining Conglomerate said they absolutely enjoy classical music. Mining companies really do have refined taste, as long as nobody brings up the ore prices.

Cracklor Logistics then raised the stakes with a link that Bob Corp recognised as To Zanarkand, from Final Fantasy X. Bob Corp also put in a word for Wandering Flame and a guitar cover of it, and Food. kept the classical side going with Chopin's Fantaisie-Impromptu.

By half one the concert had turned into a blitzball reunion, with Cracklor calling it peak and Medici, after a short search of memory, recalling that Vuroja had been on their old team. The Enquirer notes this was the most productive collaboration of the day and nobody got paid for it.

Exchange weather

Calm conditions across the exchange, with a heavy front of Welding Kits and Furniture parked over Make Stuff INC and no wind to shift it. Expect scattered turnip demand overnight, particularly under a full moon.
