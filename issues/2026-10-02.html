Pulse of the Galaxy

Bored, loud and one reset away from chaos

Two lines from Farmy McFarm Face The 7th sum up the day better than this paper could. First he quoted, approvingly, the line "It's a community with plenty of energy and not much new to spend it on, so it's spending it on itself." Then, deep in the Fed row, he shouted "EVERYONE IS SO BORED THIS IS WHAT WE'VE COME TO". In between, chat read the previous issue aloud and argued with it, had a Wordle contest, raced to solve Rubik's cubes, held a long seminar on bread, and listened to a whole company lore saga.

The real grievance is still the economy. Construction players say the Fed caps are choking their spec, Bob Corp says construction players won't take his money, Daniels Inc. spent 1.2B on repairs, and Juicy's Chemical lab has 1bil of stock stuck on the market. All that energy has to go somewhere, and today a lot of it went into the paper.

DEAR EDITOR: HOLUE SENDS THE ENQUIRER A NUMBERED COMPLAINT

The star of the last issue's front page reads it in chat, finds four problems, and lists them with timestamps attached.

By the Enquirer's complaints department, which now exists

Every newspaper hopes for a reader who reads it closely. At 07:12 the Enquirer got one. Holue's Galatic Contruction, whose recruiting record led the previous edition, came into general chat to say the subtitle was "misleading". His case was that his roll of honour kept the players who had quit in a separate section, clearly marked, so the subtitle made it look as if he was calling everyone on it great and active. Fair enough. The paper always welcomes having its homework marked in public.

so I actually did not write an all caps response, this is a lie

Holue's Galatic Contruction

Then came the numbered list. Point one accused the paper of running a "PR campaign for coss". Holue quoted Coss's actual words, "not once have I ever seen any of those names", next to the paper's summary that Coss "recognised almost none of the names", and argued that adding "almost" took "away some of the blame from his statement when I prove him wrong almost immediately". Point two dismissed the paper's "was good in June" quip as irrelevant: he had been talking about the potential of recruits who quit, so the current month didn't matter.

Point three was the strongest, and he brought receipts. He pasted the 30 September exchange in which he wrote "almost fell for the bait" and "was preparing my all caps response", and concluded: "so I actually did not write an all caps response, this is a lie". The Enquirer, which does not normally mark its own homework, notes that the log he pasted shows him preparing an all-caps reply, not sending one. On that evidence we'll give him the point, through slightly gritted teeth.

Point four disputed the word "concession". Holue said "in your reality it could be you" was "less of a concession and more of an acceptance that he (crack) lives on a completely different plane of reality and we will never see eye to eye". Which, if anything, is a bleaker truce than the one we printed. He then signed off with "anyways im done :)" and left for a French essay, which shows a work ethic this paper can only envy.

Chat loved it. Farmy McFarm Face The 7th called it "the sort of content I want from the game" and urged him not to hold back, Kentaurus Ballistics said seeing it in real time was "priceless", and Drone inc. promised him the editor would give "a fair reading". It did. When Holue asked how much say Farmy has over what goes to print, Farmy said "zero". That is correct, and also a little hurtful to the paper's delivery boy.

The recruiting feud carried on anyway. At 08:20 Frugal Factories argued that if Cracklor can recruit from GR but Holue can't recruit from TiT, "that kind of settles who is the better recruiter". Holue replied that "no one active in GR has ever been recruited to TiT", explaining that CASTA AEROSPACE and Rhodes Island had been kicked before they went. Frugal pounced on the word "active". Pear Inc. added that GR "couldnt even recruit me", Cracklor said he had "recruited myself from GR into TiT", and by 18:13 Holue was explaining that "in general I don't recruit new players". So nothing is settled, and both realities are still running.

FED UP: CHAT SPLITS OVER THE FED, LOUDLY

A row about price caps turns into hashtags, then capital letters, then nobody using full stops.

By the Enquirer's central banking correspondent

It started with repair bills. Makuria Inc lost 130m to repairs, Daniels Inc. said he had spent 1.2B on them that day, and NebulaWorks pointed out that without repairs "con guys would be unemployed". From there it took about ten minutes for general chat to decide the whole economy was up for grabs.

Fed is pretty important though to have stock on critical goods

Medici Mining Conglomerate

Farmy McFarm Face The 7th went for abolition, posting "#REMOVETHEFED" over and over and insisting that "FED NEEDS TO ADJUST WITH MARKET IF KEEPING IT". SL Refining and Makuria answered with "#SAVETHEFED", Makuria calling it a "rare SL W". Medici Mining Conglomerate made the calm case: the Fed keeps critical goods in stock, and stops new players paying $100 for rations. Daniels Inc. reckoned that without it prefabs would cost about 4k each. Farmy said that was fine.

The construction side argued that the caps aren't fair across specs. Medici refused to have words put in his mouth ("I didn't say anything about construction"), and Coss asked whether he had once warned someone that "everyone eventually leaves construction because it's horrible". The most textbook answer came from Drone inc.: the Fed is good overall, killing T1 construction profits is bad, and it should move slowly to allow gentle inflation, "Central banking 101". Mittcb noted that the devs did raise Fed cap prices a few months back, but only by 5 to 10 percent.

Later Coss proposed Fed caps, minimum prices, equal admin centres for everyone and a 5 percent daily move limit, which Bob Corp sent up as "all prices should be fixed and unchanging". Coss agreed, which may have been the joke. Nothing was decided, but the caps lock key took a beating.

BOB OFFERS CASH, CONSTRUCTION SAYS NO THANKS

ATS boss says he tried to throw money at the con industry and it threw it back.

By the Enquirer's contracts desk

Bob Corp turned up with a grievance and the spreadsheet to back it. He said he had asked construction players for daily repair contracts and found "None seemed interested". He then posted his daily maintenance needs, led by 21,158 Prefab Kits and 16,121 Construction Kits, which is not small change by anyone's count.

Not even at a discount. At a premium even. Still rejected

Bob Corp

His summary had a certain bitter poetry: construction players moan that they can't move product or raise cash, he offers cash, they say no. When it was suggested he was after discounts, he said he wanted "Not even at a discount. At a premium even. Still rejected". By his count he was down $204m on contracts that day alone.

Holue's Galatic Contruction offered the construction side's view: daily contracts are tedious, and anyone asking for discounts on amenities or construction kits is "a bigger terrorist". He also said he personally wouldn't have turned Bob down. If the con lobby wants that 21,158-prefab order, it knows where Bob lives.

COSS: DRILL PRICE CRASH NOTHING TO DO WITH ME, PROBABLY

Titanium Carbide Drill hits its cheapest since July and a former agitator says he's reformed.

By the Enquirer's heavy machinery desk

At 20:31 Coss announced "3 days of pain" on Titanium Carbide Drill, now at "dirt cheap pricing", the cheapest since the start of July. He then asked where all the outraged resex players had gone. He also agreed with himself that profits were good, and invited suppliers to send him inputs with a "love you thanks". You have to admire the cheek.

I'm a peaceful boy now

Coss

Holue asked straight out whether Coss was saying he had caused the low price. Drone inc. gave what may be the best reading of the day: "He's implying it, but not saying it". Coss's actual answer was a declaration of reform.

Earlier, Pear Inc. had credited Coss with having "single handedly rescured the structural element market" by dumping buildings, and Holue had called it "a scam" and complained that Coss did it the month he respecced. Whatever is going on with the drill, nothing in the log confirms who did it. The Enquirer notes that a peaceful boy rarely needs to announce it.

The Enquirer Says

THE ENQUIRER SAYS: THE FED IS FINE, YOUR CAPS LOCK IS NOT

This paper has watched a lot of economic debates, and today's had everything: a hashtag war, a Simpsons reference and a man spelling out what a full stop is. Somewhere under the noise were some decent arguments. Medici is right that new players need rations they can afford. Drone is right that an economy with zero inflation tends to grind. And the construction crowd have a fair complaint when one spec's caps bite much harder than everyone else's.

Our verdict: keep the Fed, let the caps move more often than the last time rats did, and give Farmy's keyboard a lie-down. And before anyone else says it, nobody is getting rations at $50.

— The Editor

SEVERAL PLAYERS TRY TO WRITE THE PAPER, PAPER WRITES ABOUT THEM

Chat realises the Enquirer exists and tries to steer it. It doesn't go well.

By the Enquirer's legal department

When Medici Mining Conglomerate said he couldn't wait to see how the paper covered the Fed row, chat took it as an invitation to give orders. SL Refining claimed the editorship, Farmy McFarm Face The 7th admitted "I CAN'T MAKE IT DO SHIIT", and Makuria Inc suggested the instructions should simply read "Buy Makuria's flux". Bold. Wrong, but bold.

A few players also posted "ads" about other people rather than themselves. The paper is happy to run anyone's notice about their own business and nobody's notice about somebody else's. Those ones went in the bin, where they will be very comfortable.

Earlier, Farmy had dangled the prospect of an apology to S.T.A.R Labs. S.T.A.R said "No it's quite alright", then "I mean if you insist". The Enquirer does not insist.

S.T.A.R LABS NAMED BOOSTING POLICE, IMMEDIATELY BRIBED

Ares Inc. hands out a badge and the officer takes the day off.

By the Enquirer's crime correspondent

After Bob Corp mentioned an ATS member who hit 2.5B in about a month, Ares Inc. decided F4T's S.T.A.R Labs was "the boosting police". The job, as Ares described it, is that if anyone is "having fun or growing too quickly", you yell in chat and get them banned.

they bribed me, so i legally cant

S.T.A.R Labs

S.T.A.R Labs seemed confused about the posting ("HOW IS IT MY JOB"), asked who exactly he was meant to be shouting at, and finally declared "they bribed me, so i legally cant". Nothing in the log suggests any bribe beyond the joke, and the paper is not filing charges against a policeman who is clearly off duty.

Chat also briefly turned on the chat filter itself, after some genuinely nasty words got through while milder ones were starred out. "love when actual slurs get through", said Makuria Inc, speaking for most of the room. The Enquirer won't be reprinting any of it, and asks the devs to take a look at the filter.

ALDERA LORE: THE CEO WHO WOULD NOT DIE

A mining company's backstory turns out to have more coups than most history books.

By the Enquirer's literary editor

What began as a question from Quimicologia about how long a 39-year-old hero lives became the evening's main event. aldera mining company revealed that, excluding one man, the company has "a 100% CEO mortality rate". That included one CEO thrown out of a window and eaten for trying to cut benefits (pies, aldera thinks).

aldera lore is wild

aldera mining company

There followed the iron faction, the council of pipes, a cult of Graphenium, a planet renamed "gulag superior", and a lone swordsman who turned out to be a child CEO taken by the Fed years earlier, who "studied the blade (and economics)". He survived a defenestration attempt thanks to a parachute, parried a stabbing, survived an RPG fired from a helicopter minus one arm, and then purged the managers who had tried to replace him. Berlin food distributions kept suggesting new ways to kill him, without success.

He has since retired and is alive at 110, the only aldera CEO not to die in office, while Nerith Kane won re-election "not even close". Drone inc. called it "gold" and has asked for the lore book. The Enquirer would buy a copy.
